Today, I'd rather write about him.

Which probably surprises absolutely nobody.

We've had a difficult couple of weeks.

Not because we stopped loving each other.

Quite the opposite.

Sometimes we -I- just manage to turn the smallest things into the biggest arguments.

And sometimes those arguments carry words neither of us truly deserves.

I'm writing this, but before I publish it, I'll probably spend some time making sure it's really okay.

Slowly.

Because I know what he asks me whenever we're hurting.

"Do you even believe in what you write?"

I do.

I think he's the one who struggles to believe it in those moments.

Maybe because pain has a way of making love look smaller than it really is.

Today, we're okay again, but I don't want "okay again" to become our routine.

I don't want us to start saying, "It's fine. It'll pass."

Because that's not what I want us to settle for.

Loving someone shouldn't mean accepting the same mistakes over and over.

I want us to stop them before they grow into something bigger.

I need to trust myself more, so I stop turning fear into damage.

And I hope he'll learn to trust what I say when I tell him I'm not leaving.

Because his place beside me has never been the question.

Never.

I don't want us to become a couple that's simply good at surviving storms. I want us to become one that learns how to stop creating them.

I know I'll never love him quietly.

I'll probably always be jealous. Possessive. Hopelessly obsessed with him.

I don't want any of that to disappear.

But I do want to love him better.

More patiently. More gently. More wisely.

Because loving him more will always be my favorite thing.

But loving him better should always be my greatest goal.

I want him to feel safe with me.

I want him to feel understood. Supported. Chosen.

I want him to feel like the most loved man in the world. Because that's who he is.

No matter how fiercely I love him, I hope he always feels loved even more.
