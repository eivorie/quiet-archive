I think I've finally managed to put my finger on something that's been hurting me more than I realized.

I failed him.

Not because he doesn't love me anymore. He told me he does. Not because he wants to learn how to live without me. I asked him that, and he said no.

But because somewhere along the way, I feel like I stopped being the person I wanted to be for him.

I wanted to take care of him.

I wanted him to have somewhere to rest after everything life had already put him through. Somewhere he wouldn't have to defend himself, explain himself, or feel like he wasn't enough. I wanted him to know what it felt like to be loved without having to earn it every single day.

I wanted to make things easier for him.

And now he's telling me he's reached a breaking point.

That he's exhausted. That he's depressed. That he needs time alone because he can't handle any more conflict.

And I can't stop wondering how much of that exhaustion has my name written all over it.

I know his struggles didn't begin with me. I know there are things he's been carrying since long before we found each other again. I know I can't be responsible for everything happening inside his head.

But knowing that doesn't make it any easier to think about the times I added to the weight.

The times I misunderstood him. The times I assumed he meant something else than what he did. The times I let my fear speak before giving him a chance to explain.

The times I made someone I love feel like he had to defend himself against me.

I hate thinking about that.

Especially because I know exactly how it feels.

We've both questioned each other's intentions. We've both allowed our insecurities to turn things that weren't meant to hurt into reasons to fight. We've both found ourselves defending things we never meant, explaining feelings we never had, trying to prove that our love was sincere.

And somehow, despite knowing how painful that is, we kept doing it.

I wish we'd known how to stop.

I wish I'd known how to stop.

There are so many moments I would handle differently if I could go back. Not because I think every concern I had was unreasonable, or because everything that happened was my fault. Some things genuinely hurt me. Some things still do.

But I wish I'd been able to separate what he actually did from what I was afraid it meant.

I wish we'd both been better at that.

There's also the other part. The part that hurts just as much.

He's struggling, and I can't help him.

Or rather, I can't help him in the way I desperately want to.

I can't reach across the ocean and hold him. I can't sit beside him in silence, make him something to eat, or simply let him rest against me without expecting him to say a word.

I can't even offer him a conversation without wondering whether I'm asking for more than he can give.

The person I want to comfort needs distance from me.

And I don't know what to do with that.

I know love isn't supposed to make me his therapist, his solution, or the person responsible for keeping him together. I know that intellectually.

But emotionally?

Emotionally, it feels like standing outside in the cold, knowing someone you love is hurting on the other side of a door you shouldn't open.

And the worst part is wondering whether I'm one of the reasons he needed to close it.

Because I don't want him to stay miserable just so I can feel needed. I want him to be okay.

I want him to wake up without feeling crushed by the thought of another day. I want him to enjoy things again. To laugh without forcing it. To have enough energy to do more than work, sleep, and wait for the next opportunity to sleep again.

I want him to feel like himself. And I hate that I can't simply love him into feeling better.

I think I've been confusing two things.

Being someone who loves him, and being someone who can protect him from everything.

I wanted to be both.

Maybe that's why his pain feels so much like my failure.

Because if I couldn't keep him from hurting, and sometimes even contributed to that hurt, then what does that say about the kind of partner I've been?

I don't have a comfortable answer.
