Today feels heavy.
Maybe it’s because I’ve been thinking too much.
Maybe it’s because loving you opens every door inside me —
the bright ones, and the dark ones too.

I’m scared.
Scared in a way I’ve never been before.
Scared because you’re the best thing that’s ever happened to me,
and losing you feels like the kind of pain I wouldn’t recover from.

I keep wondering if this is temporary for you —
if one day you’ll wake up and see that I’m not good enough,
that I’m too complicated, too intense, too fragile in all the wrong places.
I’m afraid you’ll look at me and think,
“This is too much trouble. I didn’t sign up for this.”

I know my temper is sharp when it concerns you.
I know my jealousy burns hotter than I want it to.
I know my fears sometimes spill over in ways I can’t control.
And inside those moments, I feel monstrous —
like you’ll see a version of me you don’t want,
a version you didn’t expect,
and decide that loving me is too heavy.

I’m afraid you’ll miss something I can never give you.
That the life you had before — simpler, easier, quieter —
will look tempting compared to the intensity of this.
That choosing me will one day feel like choosing difficulty
when you could have chosen peace.

Because everything with you is stronger.
All of it.
The love, the longing, the happiness —
they feel like fire in my chest.
But the fears are just as violent.
The insecurity, the jealousy, the ache of wanting you so much —
it all hits ten times harder with you.

Loving you feels like breathing and choking at the same time.
Like being held and falling, simultaneously.
Like my heart finally found its place
and immediately became terrified of losing it.

I’m overwhelmed — not because the love is too much,
but because you matter too much.
Because everything I feel for you is bigger than anything I’ve ever known.
Because you made me feel alive again,
and with that came the fear of going back to nothing.

And there is something else I’m almost ashamed to admit, but it lives in me every day: I am possessive when it comes to you. Deeply, instinctively, fiercely possessive. I can’t stand the thought of anyone reaching for you, anyone trying to pull you back into a life that no longer belongs to them. You made it clear that you’re mine — but my mind still circles the fear that someone won’t respect that.

It sits in the back of my thoughts, quiet but sharp, whispering that they might try again, that they might succeed, that I might watch you slip away because someone else demanded a piece of you that isn’t theirs anymore. It’s not jealousy for the sake of jealousy — it’s fear, real fear, the kind that tightens the chest and shakes the ribs.

I know what I have in you. I know the kind of person you are — kind, loving, warm, honest, someone who gives light just by existing. My sunshine. But sometimes your light is so bright that it lets me see, painfully clearly, exactly what I stand to lose. Exactly how devastating it would be to watch that light walk away from me.

And maybe the hardest part in all of this is admitting how incomplete I feel when you’re not here. Not in a dramatic way — not in a way that asks anything of you — but in the quiet truth that your absence changes the shape of my days. When you’re gone, the world feels thinner, like it’s missing a layer I only learned existed once you gave it to me.

I know I’m a needy person. I feel deeply, I attach intensely, and wanting you the way I do makes the loneliness sharper when you’re not here. Not because you owe me constant presence, but because you taught me what it feels like to be seen, understood, wanted — and the contrast hits hard when the day is quiet and I’m left alone with my thoughts.

It makes me see too clearly what life would feel like without you, and I hate that feeling. Not because I depend on you to exist, but because you added something to my life I never want to lose. When you’re with me, everything steadies. When you’re not, it’s like the outline of the world shifts and nothing fits quite right anymore.

This has nothing to do with trust — I trust you completely. My fears come from inside me, from the parts of me still learning how to believe that something this good won’t disappear the moment I reach out for it.

Maybe this is what happens when someone becomes your entire world:
every joy grows, every fear magnifies,
and you realize you’re not scared of loving —
you’re scared of losing.
