This weekend felt like a gift.

He planned a movie night for us on Friday. We had been talking about it for almost two weeks, trying to make it work despite the six-hour difference — checking schedules, adjusting, waiting for something that would line up properly.

And when we finally had a date, it felt bigger than it should have. Something simple, just going to the cinema, but it didn’t feel small at all. It felt like something we built together, something we were both looking forward to in the same way.

We checked the sessions and found the closest match possible — his at 1:15pm, mine at 7:35pm. It wasn’t perfect, but it was close enough to feel like we were sharing something at the same time.

And honestly, that alone made me so excited. I was thinking about it all day — not just the movie, but the fact that we made it happen, that even with the distance, we still find ways to exist in the same moment.

I had so much fun, not just because of the movie, but because of him. Knowing we were doing the same thing, thinking about each other in that moment, it made everything feel lighter. When it ended, I called him, and I walked all the way back home with him on the phone. We stayed like that for almost two hours, just talking, and it genuinely felt like five minutes. I didn’t notice the time, the distance, anything around me. It was just him. And the moment we hung up, I missed him immediately.

The more I think about it, the more I realize he made more adjustments than I did. He always does. He’s the one bending things, finding ways, making sure it works no matter what. Sometimes I catch myself wondering if that’s even normal — if it’s normal for someone to put in that much effort, that consistently, without hesitation. For me.

Because with him, it always feels like everything is possible. Like there’s always a way, no matter the distance, no matter the constraints. And I see it. I see everything he does, all the time. The effort, the intention behind even the smallest things. None of it goes unnoticed.

It’s kind of insane, how much I love this boy.

Saturday felt like something extra, something we weren’t even supposed to have. He was told not to go to work and to stay home, and somehow that turned into another night together. I call it a night because for me it always is — I’m six hours ahead — but that doesn’t change anything. We watched Shingeki, we talked, we stayed together until I got too tired to keep my eyes open.

And even then, I didn’t want to sleep. I never do when I’m with him. Every minute feels like something I don’t want to waste, like something rare that I’ve been given and not something I’m guaranteed to have. I just want to stay a little longer, always.

Even with the distance, we have our own kind of intimacy. We’re always together in some way, always talking, always sharing something. It never feels empty between us.

But that also means it’s intense. Emotionally, mentally… in every way. Once we’re good, once we’re fully in it, it just keeps going deeper. And then comes that quiet moment after.

When everything slows down again, and we both become aware of what’s missing.

The simple things. A hug after. A kiss on the forehead. A “I love you” said out loud without a screen between us. A look. Holding hands without thinking about it.

We have something real, something strong. We share moments that feel complete. But sometimes, after all of that… there’s this soft drop, where I remember we’re not actually next to each other.

He’s my gift. My luck. My happiness. My joy. He’s everything that makes my days feel full.

And sometimes he wonders if I get bored of him, if he’s too much, or if I ever see him in a negative way. I think those questions come from everything he has to carry right now — things that would make anyone feel exposed, or unsure of how they’re perceived.

But the truth is, it never happens. Not even once. There’s nothing in him that makes me step back or hesitate. Nothing that feels overwhelming in the wrong way. If anything, it’s the opposite — it’s never enough.

I don’t get tired of him. I don’t need distance from him. I don’t look at him and see something I wish was different, or something that would make me question what we have. I see someone doing his best in situations that aren’t simple, someone who still manages to think about me, protect me, and take care of how I feel even when he has so much on his own shoulders.

And if anything, that only makes me admire him more. It doesn’t change the way I see him — it reinforces it. It shows me who he really is, and that version of him… I don’t doubt it for a second. I want to be there for him. Always.

He’s not “too much.” He’s exactly everything I didn’t know I was missing.

And if anything… I could never have enough of him.
