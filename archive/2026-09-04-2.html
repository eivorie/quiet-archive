I think I understand, at least partly, what he was trying to do.

I can understand why he thought asking her would somehow reassure me.

But that's also the thing.

I never needed her to confirm anything.

The moment her opinion became part of our conversation, we'd already lost what I was trying to protect.

I never doubted him.

Not for a single minute.

I never thought he'd lied to me.

I never accused him of making anything up.

That was never the point.

The point was simply... damn it.

She leaves me nothing.

Not even a song.

Even that became something I had to share with a past that isn't mine.

That's what broke my heart.

Not honesty.

Not trust.

Just one more thing that no longer felt like ours alone.

He's exactly like me. We don't really stop to understand each other. We hear pain, and immediately turn it into guilt.

It's almost a pattern now.

One of us says, "This hurt me."

And the other immediately replies, "I'm a terrible partner."

"I'm not enough."

"I keep failing you."

Somewhere along the way, the original problem disappears completely.

Suddenly we're no longer talking about what happened. We're trying to convince each other we're not monsters.

I do it too.

Maybe we've both spent too much of our lives believing we had to become the villain every time someone else's blame needed somewhere to land.

Sometimes I'm almost afraid of saying anything.

Not because I don't trust him.

Because I already know he'll start blaming himself, he'll start looking for what he did wrong, even when that's not what I'm trying to tell him.

Maybe if I'd explained myself better, he wouldn't have misunderstood me.

Maybe he wouldn't have felt the need to ask someone else to validate something that only belonged between us.

Maybe we wouldn't be here now.

Maybe this difficult week would've simply remained... a difficult week.

See?

I always end up looking for my part in it.

Maybe that's healthier than looking for someone to blame.
