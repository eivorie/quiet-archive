Chapter I —

Eren

The steps were slow; he admired the village at dawn. From afar, the mountains seemed endless, yet the green still remained. Everything appeared calm and peaceful. With each step, the market vendors greeted him.

—Good morning, Eren!

The greeting was repeated several times along the way by different people, and his response was always the same—the reciprocal, with a gentle smile.

Eren walked a bit further until he was distracted by a child running past. He stopped in his tracks and simply looked at the older man running behind the child, a rope in his hand.

—Stop that brat! He’s stealing my goods!

Eren didn’t have much time to react, but he noticed the boy holding two apples, untouched. He simply let the child pass by.

—Are you deaf? The boy just ran past you and you do nothing? —the man, out of breath from running, glared at Eren.

Eren approached.

— I wouldn’t cause trouble with a child over two apples. —he said subtly and prepared to walk away, but was stopped by the man’s hand on his arm.

—Well, it doesn’t surprise me, this behavior coming from a Jaeger, they say your father...

Eren placed one hand behind him to reach the knife at his waist, but was stopped by a familiar voice.

— Eren! —Carla shouted her son’s name. —Come, help me with the goods… now.

Eren sighed lightly and let go of his hand, still looking the man in the eyes as if memorizing every feature of his face. Eyes shifted.

—I’m sorry, sir… it was my mistake. —He withdrew his hand from the knife and reached into his pocket for some coins. —Here… it should cover the cost of the apples.

Eren walked down but then just paused and turned to the man again .

—May I have your name, sir?

The man just looked Eren up and down.

— Patrick ,what’s the matter now ?

-Nothing, Patrick …- Eren curved his lips and then just walked away following his mother .

Levi

Morning in the castle began before the sun touched the highest towers. Levi had been awake long before that.

The infirmary carried the layered scent of crushed herbs, boiled linen, and smoke from the hearth where water simmered steadily in an iron kettle. Bundles of dried rosemary and yarrow hung from the beams above. Clay jars lined the shelves — ground willow bark, comfrey root, honey thick as amber.

Order. Cleanliness. Control.

A young soldier sat rigid on a wooden cot while Levi removed the stitches from a poorly healed gash along his forearm.

“You tore it open,” Levi said evenly. “Because you thought training mattered more than healing.”

The soldier swallowed but said nothing. Levi pressed a clean cloth soaked in diluted spirits against the wound. The man flinched.

“Next time,” Levi added calmly, “try using your judgment.”

He wrapped the arm again with fresh linen and stepped away before thanks could become conversation.

The next patient was an older maid from the inner quarters. Her hands were swollen at the joints, fingers bent slightly from years of scrubbing stone and wringing fabric.

Levi took her hands into his own. His touch was firm, assessing — but slower. He pressed along the knuckles carefully, watching her expression more than her hands.

“You’ve been ignoring the pain.”

She gave him a small, embarrassed smile. “There is always work to do.”

“There will still be work in three days.”

Her eyes widened. “Three—?”

“You’ll rest.” His tone was steady, not raised. “I’ll send an ointment for the swelling. Comfrey and honey. Apply it morning and night.”

He reached for a small ceramic jar, already prepared, and placed it gently in her hands..

“And drink the willow-bark tea,” he added after a pause. “Not just once. Properly.”

There was something quieter in his gaze then. Not warmth exactly — but awareness.

The maid nodded, almost shyly. “Thank you, Captain.”

Levi didn’t correct the title. He released her hands carefully, as though they were more fragile than she believed.

Once she left, he returned to his worktable, grinding fresh herbs with deliberate movements. Outside the narrow windows, the courtyard echoed with steel striking steel, soldiers already in motion.

Levi did not look. His world remained contained within these walls.

Measured doses. Precise incisions. Pain endured. Pain reduced. He was useful, necessary. And, as always — Unseen.

Eren

Eren approached his mother’s small medicinal herb stall. She was silent, carefully arranging the leaves on the table with full concentration.

He then moved to open the boxes and help her, only to find them empty. He looked around and then returned to the counter, noticing the scarce merchandise. Before Eren could say anything, his mother spoke up.

—No need to look, Eren, this is all we have to sell today. —She said calmly.

—Mother, what do you mean this is all we have to sell today? We only have a few sprigs of rosemary and basil here, this won’t amount to anything, and… —He was interrupted.

—Eren… you need to understand that the weather is changing, the lands here aren’t as good anymore, we lost practically everything in the last storm, and we’re no longer receiving help. We have to manage with what we have. We’re not the only ones around here.

—And because of that, we all just have to accept this misery and be happy? Is that what you’re trying to tell me? —He said, looking at her.

Carla then met Eren’s gaze.

—You need to accept reality and stop thinking that your temperament will change the world, Eren. Your father thought the same way you do, and look at the situation he’s in…

—Mom..

—Eren… answer me. What were you trying to do just now? —She crossed her arms and stared at him.

—What are you talking about? —He asked.

—I had the impression that you tried to grab your knife… —Carla’s gaze grew sharper.

Eren chuckled softly.

—Is that serious? You really think I would do that? Is this how you want to convince me that my place is here? That, besides being content with what they give us, I also need my mother to think I’m losing my mind?

—That’s not what I meant, my son.

—It’s exactly what you meant, mom. —Eren stepped closer. —But don’t worry, I’ll find a solution for all of this, even if I have to replant every single seed one by one. I’m sorry…but I don’t agree with the way you see things. —He held Carla’s face and kissed her forehead.

—Hey! Where are you going now, Eren?

He didn’t answer and only smiled gently at her, stepping away.

Levi

The infirmary door opened briefly, letting in a current of colder air.

A servant entered carrying two crates and placed them near Levi’s table with a respectful bow.

“Fresh supplies from the southern estates, Captain.”

Levi glanced down.

Bundles of rosemary. Sage tied neatly with twine. Dried chamomile. Lavender wrapped in clean parchment. More than enough to last weeks.

He dismissed the servant with a nod and crouched beside the crates, inspecting the leaves between his fingers. Quality harvest. Well preserved, most likely recently dried.

He separated what would be used for infusions from what would be ground into paste. His movements were economical, practiced.

A small kettle hung above the hearth, steam rising gently. He added crushed willow bark and a pinch of mint before covering it again.

Footsteps approached hesitantly. It was the same maid from earlier, lingering at the doorway.

“You forgot something?” Levi asked without looking up.

She hesitated. “No, Captain. I just… wanted to thank you again.”

He finally lifted his gaze.

“Rest,” he repeated simply.

Her shoulders softened at the firmness in his voice. She nodded and left.

Outside, the castle functioned as it always did — armored boots crossing the courtyard, orders shouted from the training grounds, commanders correcting stances.

He knew those rhythms once.

He had lived by them.

Now he measured time differently — in pulses, in fevers breaking, in wounds closing.

Levi reached for another bundle of rosemary and paused briefly.

The scent carried faintly on the steam rising from the kettle.

He wasn’t sure why it unsettled him, but what else than just continue working.

Eren

It was already dark; the path to the village was dimly lit, with only a few torches burning. The view was difficult, but the village seemed calm, the sounds of nature and the echo of the mountains blending with the muffled noise of the Ashvale Tavern. The lively Scottish music grew clearer with each step the boy took. Eren pulled a kind of black cloth from his pocket and wrapped it around his face, leaving only his green eyes visible.

Eren entered the space; the tavern was very dark, the firelight creating a cozy atmosphere that contrasted sharply with the crowd, who appeared extremely drunk.

The boy carefully observed the space and nodded to Connie, the bartender, who only smiled discreetly.

—A shot to relax today? —He said, sensing Eren approaching the counter.

—No, thank you. Today is just business. —He replied quietly, looking around until he slipped into the hallway and quietly entered a back door, which could easily be mistaken for a kitchen.

At the first door, he came across Tony, a burly man stationed at that exit.

—They’re already waiting for you. —That was all the man said as Eren didn’t respond and hurried toward the next door.

The small room looked much like the tavern: low light, fire on the side providing illumination, a central table with some chairs and armchairs scattered around, where three people were seated.

—We need to be quick today; the tavern is very busy. —Eren said, looking at the three in front of him, who also had cloths covering their faces.

—Straight to the point. We got the information you wanted. There is indeed a large medicinal herb plantation associated with the province of Lyrhoria, but access is very difficult. We’re going to have a lot of work to get there, and I’m not sure if the horses can make it through the shortened path. —One of them said, showing a roughly drawn map.

—We’ll have to find a solution. The village is practically out of any supplements; they have a massive plantation for the use of ten people, and we have a tiny one for the entire village. We have no other choice. I need this urgently. Leave this map with me, and I’ll study the situation further. —

The door was violently thrown open, and Eren immediately drew the sword from his back while the other three promptly did the same.

It was another of the guards, but with a man who had a sack of potatoes tied around his neck.

—I can’t breathe! —He muttered. —Please!!

—Here you go, boss… —The guard said, setting the man in the center of the room.

Eren quickly assessed the situation while the man continued muttering in panic.

—Everyone is free. I need to speak alone with Patrick.

Levi

The castle rarely slept.

But Levi did — when he could.

That night, it lasted less than two hours.

A sharp knock struck his chamber door.

He opened his eyes immediately.

Another knock.

“Captain. Forgive the hour.”

Levi sat up, already awake in the way only former soldiers ever truly were. He swung his legs off the bed and pulled his shirt over his shoulders before unlocking the door.

A young page stood there, nervous.

“Her Grace requests your presence. Immediately.”

Levi stared at him.

“Is she dying?”

“…No, sir.”

“Bleeding?”

“No, sir.”

A pause.

“She says she cannot breathe properly.”

Levi exhaled once through his nose.

“Of course she can’t.”

He still grabbed his coat and followed.

The noble wing was warm, lit with too many candles, heavy with perfume and spiced wine. Horrendous carpets muffled his steps.

Inside the chamber, Lady Reiss lay dramatically against embroidered pillows, one hand pressed to her chest.

“It’s dreadful,” she murmured. “My heart is racing.”

Levi approached, unimpressed and took her wrist. Her pulse was steady.

“You drank?” he asked flatly.

“A little.”

“How much is a little?”

She avoided his eyes.

Levi released her hand and walked to the nearby table, pouring water into a cup before adding crushed mint and a few drops of diluted tincture.

“You’re not dying,” he said calmly. “You’re indulgent.”

She gasped softly at the tone. He handed her the cup.

“Drink. Slowly.”

She obeyed. Within minutes, her breathing evened.

“Will you stay?” she asked faintly.

“No.”

He turned and left before dramatics could trap him in conversation.The corridors were silent at this hour as he walked back alone. Outside the high windows, the night stretched far beyond the castle walls — dark land, distant villages, unseen lives.

Levi slowed near one of the windows. From here, the world looked orderly. Torches aligned along the courtyard. Guards positioned at measured intervals. The castle breathed safely within its stone ribs. Beyond the outer walls, however, the land faded into shadow. He knew what lived in those shadows. Injuries that went untreated. Fevers without medicine. Wounds left to rot because no one with skill ever arrived.

Once, he had thought learning to heal would matter. Once, he had believed usefulness meant freedom.

His reflection stared back at him faintly in the glass — candlelight cutting sharp lines along his face. He had chosen this path, chosen to remain, chosen to serve where he could do the most good.

And yet—

He spent his nights calming wine-sick nobles while entire provinces survived on whatever scraps they could grow.

The torches below burned steadily. A golden cage still counted as a cage.

Levi’s expression didn’t change.

He turned away from the window and walked back.

Eren

The door closed, and Eren found himself facing the man, still with his face covered and hands bound. He remained silent as he watched the man struggle for air.

—How do you know my name, idiot? I can’t breathe, get me out of here. —The man’s tone was desperate and gasping.

Eren removed his own black cloth, feeling his face free, knowing the man couldn’t see him.

Eren stood and leaned against his table, standing directly in front of the seated man.

—I know your name because you’re a person hard to forget. Don’t you feel important? —Eren smiled ironically, aware that even though they couldn’t see each other, his tone made his words easy to imagine.

—What do you want with me? I can’t breathe! Who are you? —Patrick coughed slightly between words.

—Unfortunately, I won’t be giving you any information, but I hope this moment will remind you of something. —Eren rested his hands on the table, getting more comfortable as he watched the other.

—You can get straight to the point, I can’t breathe! —

—Ah, of course! That was probably the same feeling your wife had the night you came back drunk from this tavern and decided it was a good time to try and suffocate her, wasn’t it? How does that feel? —

—Ha…how… —

—How do I know this? I’ve been observing you in the village for months: the way you treat people, the hypocrisy of expecting loyalty when you can’t even maintain it at home. Do you really think I don’t know you steal goods, mistreat your sick wife, humiliate others, and slander everyone so the other vendors have a bad reputation, Mr. Patrick? —Eren pulled the knife from his pocket and began twirling it between his fingers.

—What are your last words? I’m about to give your wife some relief.

—Hey… no… Look, it was a mistake, I’m not that kind of person, and—

—You’re talking too much for someone who can still breathe. —Eren pressed the sharp knife against the man’s throat, just enough to tear part of the sack of potatoes on his face, but not enough to pierce his skin.

—Come on, Patrick, tell me what your family needs to continue trading while they deal with you? —Eren applied more pressure.

—No! Listen, please, I beg you. —He said desperately. —My daughter… I have a daughter who is very sick, and my wife won’t be able to manage everything alone. Please, you can continue observing me, but not now, or if you do, you’ll destroy all of us. —He coughed desperately while crying. Eren kept the knife in place, staring coldly at the man.

—Tony! —Eren shouted, and saw the man at the door spring into action.

—What is he saying… that he has a sick daughter and his wife isn’t well either? Is that true? —Eren didn’t take his eyes off the man.

—Yes, Boss. Indeed, his daughter is near death, and the wife doesn’t look well. We’ve been observing during the time you requested. —

Eren stood still for a moment, then removed the knife, placing it on the table.

—Take this idiot to the farthest point from the village. In the meantime, have one of our men continue monitoring him. One more slip, and he won’t have another chance here. —

—Yes, sir. —Tony stepped in to take the man away.

Eren watched as the man was removed, still struggling for air. He sighed heavily, then sat back in the chair, picking up the map and studying it.

—Province of Lythoria… I guess not everything here is lost. —He gave a small smile and picked up some papers with ink, beginning to make notes.

Levi

Dawn broke pale over the castle walls.

Levi was already awake. He didn’t wait for pages to bring reports or servants to fetch supplies. When something concerned medicine, he preferred seeing it himself and the southern herbal estate had sent word of inconsistent yields. Something like soil damage after the last storm. He intended to confirm it.

A small escort waited at the gate, but he waved off half of them.

“I’m not inspecting a battlefield,” he said flatly. “Two is enough.”

The ride down from the castle was quiet. Morning air carried the scent of damp earth and crushed grass. The land stretched wide beyond the fortified walls — less polished, less controlled. When they reached the plantation, Levi dismounted without assistance.

Rows of cultivated herbs lined the sloped terrain — rosemary, sage, feverfew, mint. Larger plots farther down held comfrey and foxglove under careful watch.

He crouched near one section, running soil through his fingers. Too compact.

He stood and walked the perimeter slowly. Something felt wrong. Not ruined, not destroyed, but wrong.

A rope mark along one fence post. Slightly loosened.

Boot impressions near the northern boundary — not from the estate workers’ usual path.

Levi crouched again, brushing dirt aside with two fingers. Fresh enough.

One of the guards shifted uneasily. “Problem, Captain?”

Levi stood.

“Not yet.”

His gaze lifted toward the tree line beyond the plantation. The forest edge remained still. Quiet. Too quiet for this early hour.

The estate overseer approached nervously. “We’ve had no theft, sir. Everything is accounted for.”

“I didn’t ask about theft.”

The man swallowed. Levi walked further down the rows, pausing beside a patch of willow saplings. He cut a small branch cleanly with a short blade and examined the bark.

Good quality, abundant. Enough here to treat fevers in three provinces.

He glanced once more toward the distant hills.

Levi adjusted his gloves.

“Double the watch rotations at night,” he ordered calmly. “And reinforce the northern fence.”

“Yes, Captain.”

He mounted his horse again.

As they rode back toward the castle, the wind shifted. It carried the faintest scent of tavern smoke from a distant village. Levi did not know why the direction registered in his mind. But he remembered it.

Eren

For days, several meetings were held at the tavern. The group was not large, but their connections with other provinces meant they were spread far and wide. Two more villages gradually joined the plan, so Ashvale would have the excuse of obtaining crops from neighboring villages, reducing suspicion if the royal guard began investigations.

As always, Eren prepared for every possible scenario and chose to observe closely for several weeks. He paid attention to every celebration, festival, event, or unusual movement that could be related to the royalty.

The plan was already set in motion. There was no longer any way to endure the shortage—not only of medicine, but of food across the region. The plan was complex, and there would be consequences, but there were no second options.

They would take small amounts at a time, intelligently, without drawing attention at first, and from the areas farthest from the castle, considering the vastness of the lands that belonged to them. One of the key elements was spreading false word that a blight had infested the soil—not difficult to believe, given the village’s current reality.

—So you’re going to steal from them tomorrow? —Armin said as Eren pulled on his shirt after bathing.

—Keep your voice down. And listen, I’m not going to steal from them, I’m taking what’s ours. Do you think they care? They don’t even have time to check the size of those plantations. —Eren said as he dressed.

—Don’t you think you’re going too far? What if the view your father gave you about the royalty is completely wrong, Eren?

—It’s not the view he gave me, it’s what’s right in front of my eyes, Armin. They limit supplies to the villages while they have exclusive treatment for every fainting spell their Highness suffers. —Eren’s comment was serious, but it made Armin laugh.

—I still think this won’t work, Eren. You’re very good at controlling the village and everything that happens here, but you don’t know who stands behind what happens there. —He said, this time looking at Eren seriously.

—Great. Next time, I’ll find a better best friend who believes in my potential. —Eren said, finishing getting dressed.

—You look very handsome, if that helps at all. Are you trying to find someone at the Lantern Festival? —Armin teased.

—I’m looking for peace tonight and a drink or two. Let’s enjoy this festival before I end our friendship, please. —Eren laughed softly, shaking his head.

Levi

Levi was midway through reorganizing a shelf of dried feverfew when the infirmary door burst open without warning.

“I have excellent news!” Hanji announced far too loudly for the hour.

Levi didn’t look up.

“If it involves blood, broken bones, or an outbreak, say it quietly.”

“It involves lanterns.”

That made him pause.

He turned slowly.

Hanji stood in the doorway, hair half-tied, ink smudged along one sleeve, grin wide enough to be suspicious.

“The Lantern Festival begins tonight. Her Highness insisted on a grand opening this year. Speeches. Music. Public appearance. The works.”

Levi blinked once.

“And why,” he asked flatly, “is that excellent news?”

“Because,” Hanji said, stepping further inside and leaning against his table, “you are required to attend.”

Silence.

Levi stared at her.

“No.”

“Oh yes,” she said cheerfully. “Royal presence. Visible security. You’re the crown’s sharpest blade, remember? They like displaying that.”

“I’m not a decoration.”

“You are tonight.”
