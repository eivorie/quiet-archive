Chapter VII

Levi

For a fraction of a second, Levi froze.

Not because Eren was kissing him.

Because none of it made sense.

The warning. The knife. Eren's hand still hovering over his own weapon. The sudden seriousness in his eyes, followed by an invitation to put a blade through his back if Levi decided he'd gone too far.

Levi had spent too many years surviving by noticing when pieces didn't fit.

And these didn't.

Still, Eren's mouth was against his, fingers threaded into the short hair at his nape, and Levi's body betrayed his analysis by responding before he'd reached a conclusion.

He kissed him back.

His knife remained in his hand.

Levi didn't close his eyes.

His gaze shifted past Eren instead, searching what little of the street he could see beyond his shoulder. The crowd. The entrances between buildings. Anyone standing still while everyone else watched the fireworks. Anyone watching them instead.

Was this what Eren had seen?

Someone he didn't want recognizing him? Someone he didn't want seeing his face? Or had Eren simply found the most deranged possible way to distract Levi from something happening behind him?

His free hand closed around Eren's side, ostensibly pulling him closer, though the position gave Levi a better angle past him.

And that was the problem.

Because even while every instinct told him there was something else happening, the kiss itself didn't feel quite as easy to dismiss as part of whatever game Eren was playing.

Levi broke it first, but barely moved away. From a distance, there would be little difference; Eren's hand was still at his neck, Levi's remained against him, their faces separated by inches.

His voice was low enough for Eren alone.

— Who are we hiding from?

Eren

Eren’s hands remained at the back of Levi’s neck. His breathing was slightly uneven as he rested his forehead against Levi’s.

“I’m sorry… I don’t know what came over me, and—”

“Eren!”

Armin appeared, slightly out of breath. Eren immediately pulled away from Levi and turned toward him.

Eren looked at Armin in confusion, but from the look in his eyes, he could tell that Armin wasn’t surprised merely by what he had just witnessed. Something else was happening.

Eren glanced back at Levi.

“I have to go.”

He stepped away and followed Armin with long strides until the two of them broke into a run toward the tavern. Eren could feel his blood beginning to boil. Armin had given him very little information, and Eren entered the tavern impatiently, kicking the door to their hideout open with force.

Jean was there.

“Stressed?” he laughed.

“What the hell are you doing?!” Eren snapped impatiently.

“We’re not doing anything. You know that when Connie drinks, no one can control him. He was drunk and decided to go on an adventure, Eren. What’s the problem?”

Jean stood up.

“We looked all over this village for you, and where the hell were you? I’m not going to sit here babysitting everyone. It’s not my job—just like it’s not my job to protect fucking royal soldiers!”

Eren let out a heavy breath. As strong as his urge to punch Jean was, he forced himself to remain focused.

“What did Connie say he was going to do, Jean?”

“I don’t know. But apparently, he wants to play hero for Zeke and go up against some of the royal soldiers patrolling around the kingdom. Maybe steal some of the crops or something? I don’t know, idiot!”

Jean took another drink.

“Do you realize he could get himself killed? No backup, nothing. He’s drunk, Jean. He could get all of us killed…”

Eren’s nerves were on edge, but he knew arguing wouldn’t accomplish anything. He turned and stormed out of the tavern.

“Tony! Get reinforcements! Southern part of the forest, heading toward the castle… NOW!”

Eren yelled as he grabbed his mask and pulled it over his face, heading toward the back exit in search of his horse.

He quickly mounted and rode off.

For a brief second, Levi’s eyes flashed through his mind, and somehow, Eren doubted it would be the last time he saw them again that night.

“Damn it…”

*

The soldiers spoke calmly among themselves as they carried out their patrol.

One of the horses approached Levi and the rest of the group.

“Captain Levi. Suspicious activity in the forest. We don’t know exactly what’s happening, but I believe they spotted a small group of masked individuals heading toward the castle…”

The soldier paused.

“We need reinforcements.”

Levi

Levi didn't move. He watched Eren disappear into the crowd after Armin, the knife still hanging loosely from his hand.

For several seconds, that was all he did. Then he looked down at the blade. Apparently, they hadn't been hiding from anyone. Levi slowly slid it back into its sheath.

— Unbelievable.

The word disappeared beneath another burst of fireworks.

Eren had kissed him, apologized for it before Levi could even decide what the hell he thought about that, and then vanished after Armin without so much as an explanation.

Levi wasn't following him. Whatever emergency had just dragged Eren away belonged to Eren unless someone decided to make it Levi's problem. He'd spent enough of the past two days being pulled from one unexplained situation into another.

He had eventually returned to his patrol with the lingering warmth of Eren's mouth still irritatingly difficult to ignore.

Now, standing among his men, it disappeared beneath something far more familiar.

Masked individuals.

Levi's expression changed immediately.

— How many?

— We couldn't get an exact count. Four, maybe five. They were moving quickly.

Toward the castle.

Only minutes ago, Eren had been standing in front of him with his hand on a knife. Then Armin had appeared, Eren had taken one look at him and left without explanation.

He didn't like coincidences.

He liked this one even less.

But suspicion wasn't certainty, and he wasn't about to gamble his soldiers' lives on either.

— Send word to the nearest patrol. No one goes into that forest alone.

His voice carried easily over the remaining noise of the festival.

— Two men stay here. The rest come with me.

The soldiers moved immediately.

Levi mounted, gathering the reins into one hand. His gaze settled briefly on the dark line of trees beyond the village.

He had no idea what Eren was doing out there.

That bothered him considerably more now than it would have an hour ago.

The memory of fingers at the back of his neck surfaced at precisely the wrong moment.

Levi clicked his tongue.

Not now.

— Move.
