Chapter VII

Levi

For a fraction of a second, Levi froze.

Not because Eren was kissing him.

Because none of it made sense.

The warning. The knife. Eren's hand still hovering over his own weapon. The sudden seriousness in his eyes, followed by an invitation to put a blade through his back if Levi decided he'd gone too far.

Levi had spent too many years surviving by noticing when pieces didn't fit.

And these didn't.

Still, Eren's mouth was against his, fingers threaded into the short hair at his nape, and Levi's body betrayed his analysis by responding before he'd reached a conclusion.

He kissed him back.

His knife remained in his hand.

Levi didn't close his eyes.

His gaze shifted past Eren instead, searching what little of the street he could see beyond his shoulder. The crowd. The entrances between buildings. Anyone standing still while everyone else watched the fireworks. Anyone watching them instead.

Was this what Eren had seen?

Someone he didn't want recognizing him? Someone he didn't want seeing his face? Or had Eren simply found the most deranged possible way to distract Levi from something happening behind him?

His free hand closed around Eren's side, ostensibly pulling him closer, though the position gave Levi a better angle past him.

And that was the problem.

Because even while every instinct told him there was something else happening, the kiss itself didn't feel quite as easy to dismiss as part of whatever game Eren was playing.

Levi broke it first, but barely moved away. From a distance, there would be little difference; Eren's hand was still at his neck, Levi's remained against him, their faces separated by inches.

His voice was low enough for Eren alone.

— Who are we hiding from?

Eren

Eren’s hands remained at the back of Levi’s neck. His breathing was slightly uneven as he rested his forehead against Levi’s.

“I’m sorry… I don’t know what came over me, and—”

“Eren!”

Armin appeared, slightly out of breath. Eren immediately pulled away from Levi and turned toward him.

Eren looked at Armin in confusion, but from the look in his eyes, he could tell that Armin wasn’t surprised merely by what he had just witnessed. Something else was happening.

Eren glanced back at Levi.

“I have to go.”

He stepped away and followed Armin with long strides until the two of them broke into a run toward the tavern. Eren could feel his blood beginning to boil. Armin had given him very little information, and Eren entered the tavern impatiently, kicking the door to their hideout open with force.

Jean was there.

“Stressed?” he laughed.

“What the hell are you doing?!” Eren snapped impatiently.

“We’re not doing anything. You know that when Connie drinks, no one can control him. He was drunk and decided to go on an adventure, Eren. What’s the problem?”

Jean stood up.

“We looked all over this village for you, and where the hell were you? I’m not going to sit here babysitting everyone. It’s not my job—just like it’s not my job to protect fucking royal soldiers!”

Eren let out a heavy breath. As strong as his urge to punch Jean was, he forced himself to remain focused.

“What did Connie say he was going to do, Jean?”

“I don’t know. But apparently, he wants to play hero for Zeke and go up against some of the royal soldiers patrolling around the kingdom. Maybe steal some of the crops or something? I don’t know, idiot!”

Jean took another drink.

“Do you realize he could get himself killed? No backup, nothing. He’s drunk, Jean. He could get all of us killed…”

Eren’s nerves were on edge, but he knew arguing wouldn’t accomplish anything. He turned and stormed out of the tavern.

“Tony! Get reinforcements! Southern part of the forest, heading toward the castle… NOW!”

Eren yelled as he grabbed his mask and pulled it over his face, heading toward the back exit in search of his horse.

He quickly mounted and rode off.

For a brief second, Levi’s eyes flashed through his mind, and somehow, Eren doubted it would be the last time he saw them again that night.

“Damn it…”

*

The soldiers spoke calmly among themselves as they carried out their patrol.

One of the horses approached Levi and the rest of the group.

“Captain Levi. Suspicious activity in the forest. We don’t know exactly what’s happening, but I believe they spotted a small group of masked individuals heading toward the castle…”

The soldier paused.

“We need reinforcements.”

Levi

Levi didn't move. He watched Eren disappear into the crowd after Armin, the knife still hanging loosely from his hand.

For several seconds, that was all he did. Then he looked down at the blade. Apparently, they hadn't been hiding from anyone. Levi slowly slid it back into its sheath.

— Unbelievable.

The word disappeared beneath another burst of fireworks.

Eren had kissed him, apologized for it before Levi could even decide what the hell he thought about that, and then vanished after Armin without so much as an explanation.

Levi wasn't following him. Whatever emergency had just dragged Eren away belonged to Eren unless someone decided to make it Levi's problem. He'd spent enough of the past two days being pulled from one unexplained situation into another.

He had eventually returned to his patrol with the lingering warmth of Eren's mouth still irritatingly difficult to ignore.

Now, standing among his men, it disappeared beneath something far more familiar.

Masked individuals.

Levi's expression changed immediately.

— How many?

— We couldn't get an exact count. Four, maybe five. They were moving quickly.

Toward the castle.

Only minutes ago, Eren had been standing in front of him with his hand on a knife. Then Armin had appeared, Eren had taken one look at him and left without explanation.

He didn't like coincidences.

He liked this one even less.

But suspicion wasn't certainty, and he wasn't about to gamble his soldiers' lives on either.

— Send word to the nearest patrol. No one goes into that forest alone.

His voice carried easily over the remaining noise of the festival.

— Two men stay here. The rest come with me.

The soldiers moved immediately.

Levi mounted, gathering the reins into one hand. His gaze settled briefly on the dark line of trees beyond the village.

He had no idea what Eren was doing out there.

That bothered him considerably more now than it would have an hour ago.

The memory of fingers at the back of his neck surfaced at precisely the wrong moment.

Levi clicked his tongue.

Not now.

— Move.

Eren

Eren galloped quickly through the forest, the moonlight illuminating the dark path ahead. His eyes remained focused. He decided to take the middle trail, where it would be easier to meet up with the reinforcements that were on their way.

Eren’s group knew exactly what to do in situations like this. They split up strategically, using shortcuts that only they knew about. Since they had very little information about the path Connie had taken, they had no other option but to rely on luck.

Eren reached down to touch his belt, making sure his knife was still in the same position. It felt as though he had suffered a momentary lapse of memory, but the thoughts kept forcing their way back into his mind at exactly the wrong moment.

Focus.

That was what he kept telling himself.

After a few minutes, Eren heard a faint disturbance nearby. He remained alert, but then a familiar voice echoed through the forest, followed by a whistle signaling that the reinforcements had arrived.

The horses quickly made their way toward one another, and soon the group was nearly reunited.

“When we find Connie, your priority is making sure the other soldiers don’t get close to him. It won’t be easy. Apparently, Connie is too drunk to defend himself, so I’ll take care of that. They’ll do everything they can to at least capture him, so listen to me—under no circumstances can we let that happen.”

Eren spoke loudly, his voice carrying through the forest.

“We’ll keep the other soldiers occupied, but you’ll be their main focus, Eren. They’ll notice we have one soldier who’s not exactly in any condition to fight.”

Marco said as he adjusted his mask.

“I hope they don’t kill Connie, because I’ll do it with my own hands…”

Eren took a deep breath.

“Fucking irresponsible.”

“Boss, there he is.”

One of them pointed ahead.

In the distance, they could vaguely make out a horse moving erratically and without much control, while Connie clung to it, an arrow in his hand.

Then Eren faintly heard more horses approaching.

Royalty.

Soldiers.

Levi.

“Awesome.”

He muttered in frustration, clicking his tongue.

Levi

Levi heard the horse before he saw it, hooves striking the ground unevenly somewhere ahead. He raised one hand and his soldiers slowed immediately, spreading across the trail just as a rider emerged between the trees.

The man could barely stay in the saddle. One hand fought uselessly with the reins while the other clutched a bow, an arrow already being dragged clumsily into place.

— Stop.

The rider looked toward them and drew anyway.

Levi's expression didn't change. His bow was raised, drawn and released in one smooth motion. His arrow struck Connie's before he could loose it properly, knocking it violently aside and sending it into the undergrowth.

— Get him off that horse.

Two soldiers moved forward.

Then came the sound of approaching hooves. Levi's men immediately raised their weapons as several masked figures emerged between the trees.

Levi recognized Eren at once.

The knife. The kiss. Armin appearing seconds later. Eren disappearing without explanation. And now Eren was here, masked and armed, arriving with reinforcements for the man who had just tried to fire on a royal patrol.

For Levi, the pieces finally fit.

Whatever had remained in his expression disappeared. His features settled into something cold and perfectly still, his eyes emptied of even the irritation that had followed him into the forest. There was nothing in them now for Eren to read.

— Surround them. Weapons on the ground. All of you.

His soldiers spread out, cutting off the path behind the masked group while the others secured Connie.

Levi dismounted. His gaze travelled slowly across the men in front of him, assessing weapons, numbers, positions. When it reached Eren, it moved straight past him without the slightest pause.

— Anyone who resists gets put down. Take the masks off and detain them.

Then Levi walked forward.

He passed close enough to Eren that ignoring him became an act in itself. Levi's eyes remained fixed ahead, cold and distant. No recognition. No anger. Not even the courtesy of using his name.

Nothing.

Only another masked man under arrest.

Whatever Eren had to say this time, Levi wasn't interested in asking.

Eren

Eren’s breathing was slightly heavy, but he couldn’t let it show.

He looked at Levi.

Not a single glance was returned to him. He was simply ignored.

Levi didn’t know what was happening there, yet he didn’t even bother to look at Eren. Eren felt impatient—and, in some way, disappointed.

“Anyone who resists gets put down.”

The command came from the same mouth Eren had kissed barely an hour ago.

There was no consideration. Not even the acknowledgment that Eren’s group hadn’t hurt anyone.

Cold.

Eren finally looked away from Levi, fixing his eyes on some distant point instead.

“Release the rest of the soldiers, and I’ll surrender.”

He spoke firmly.

“One of the men is not in any condition to fight. We came here with no intention other than bringing him back safe and sound.”

Levi

Levi didn't look at him.

Not when Eren offered himself in exchange for the others, and not when he claimed they had come only to bring their companion back safely.

His expression remained cold and empty, his eyes fixed on the soldiers securing the man who had nearly fired on them moments earlier.

— Those of you who still feel inclined to negotiate should take a good look at the man being restrained over there.

His voice remained perfectly level.

— He aimed a loaded bow at my men and tried to fire. The only reason no one was hit is because I stopped the arrow.

Levi adjusted the leather of his glove, still without sparing Eren a glance.

— So if anyone else would like to explain to me how harmless your intentions are, choose your next move very carefully.

His eyes swept across the masked group.

Around Eren. Past Eren.

Never at him.

— Mikasa. Hanji. Erwin.

The three names were spoken calmly.

— Disarm every one of them. Remove their masks, bind their hands and take them all into custody. If anyone resists, put them down.

There would be no bargains. No exchanges. No exceptions.

Levi turned away.

As far as he was concerned, the discussion was over.

Eren

Eren’s heart began to race, though he forced himself to remain calm as his hands started to sweat.

He wasn’t entirely sure what was making him feel that way—whether it was the adrenaline or Levi’s coldness.

There was no trust. None at all.

“He was there… the fireworks… the drinks…”

That was all Eren said. He was referring to the presence of someone who knew about what had happened an hour earlier.

“We won’t harm you if you don’t harm us… I’m asking again. Release them.”

He tried once more.

The soldiers came to a stop, waiting for Levi’s command, their expressions carrying a hint of confusion.

Levi

No one stopped.

Mikasa reached the first man and removed his weapon without hesitation while Hanji moved to the next. Erwin directed the remaining soldiers around the group, closing every possible route out of the clearing. Levi's orders had been clear. Eren repeating himself changed nothing.

— You were given an order. Carry it out.

That was all it took.

One by one, weapons were taken, masks removed and wrists bound. Connie was already secured, barely steady on his feet, while the others were placed under guard. Whatever arrangement Eren thought he could negotiate had ceased to exist the moment one of their arrows had been aimed at Levi's men.

Only when the soldiers reached Eren did Levi's gaze finally settle on him.

Empty.

Eren looked like he still had something to say. Levi had heard enough half-sentences for one night.

— Separate him from the others.

His eyes remained on Eren for another second before shifting to Erwin.

— The rest go to the cells. No one speaks to each other on the way back.

Then, finally:

— I'll deal with him separately.

Eren

Eren looked around as he watched his companions being quickly apprehended. His eyes stung with the beginnings of tears from sheer anger, but he took a deep breath and forced his expression back into something serious. His gaze never wavered, even when he heard Connie’s uncontrollable laughter.

“I warned you, idiot!”

Connie continued laughing as he could barely keep his balance under the grip of the soldier holding him.

“Idiooot…”

He repeated, looking directly at Eren.

Eren said nothing.

He simply needed to wait. But in some way, he wasn’t willing to deal with Levi.

Levi

The journey back to the castle was quiet.

The prisoners were kept under close guard, their horses led rather than ridden, weapons confiscated and hands bound. Connie's occasional drunken mumbling was the only thing that disturbed the steady sound of hooves against the road.

Levi rode at the front.

He didn't look back.

By the time the castle came into view, the last traces of the festival had disappeared behind them. The gates opened at Levi's approach, and the night watch quickly cleared the courtyard as the prisoners were brought inside.

— Search them again. Everything comes off them before they enter the cells.

The order was carried out immediately. Knives, concealed weapons and anything else they might use were removed one by one.

Levi's attention caught briefly when a guard pulled the knife from Eren's belt.

Feel free to stab me in the back.

The words returned with unpleasant clarity.

Levi's eyes rested on the blade for a moment before moving away.

Funny.

Of the two of them, Eren seemed to have managed that perfectly well without one.

— Separate him from the others.

He didn't need to specify who.

Eren was taken farther down the corridor while the rest of the group disappeared one by one behind iron doors. Levi's eyes remained on the guards rather than the prisoners.

— No visitors. No conversations between cells. Two guards on the corridor until morning.

The last door closed with a heavy metallic sound.

Only then did Levi turn away.

There would be no interrogation tonight. They were tired, tempers were high, and at least one of them was too drunk to give his own name without laughing about it.

They could sleep on stone for a few hours.

Morning would be soon enough.

Eren

About thirty minutes had passed when one of the guards went to find Levi.

He knocked on the door to Levi’s quarters.

The door opened.

“I know I shouldn’t be bothering you, but it’s practically impossible…the guy…”

He paused.

“...the drunk one…”

Another pause.

“…he insists on speaking with you.”

Levi

Levi stared at the guard for a long moment.

He had barely removed his jacket, and whatever patience he'd possessed at the beginning of the evening had been left somewhere between the village, the forest and the castle gates.

— No.

The guard hesitated.

— Captain, he really—

— I heard you. I'm not having a midnight conversation with a drunk prisoner because he's decided he needs an audience.

Levi rubbed a hand over his face and stepped back toward the door.

— Give him some chamomile.

The guard blinked.

Levi's expression remained perfectly serious.

— Apparently it's good to calm down.

He began closing the door.

— If he's still desperate to talk when he's sober, he can wait until morning like everyone else.

The guard wisely didn't knock again.

For the first time in what felt like days, the castle eventually fell silent. No alarms followed, no urgent footsteps crossed the corridor outside Levi's quarters, and no one else appeared at his door demanding his attention.

Levi slept.

Not nearly enough, but deeply enough that when morning light finally crept through the narrow windows of his quarters, the previous night returned to him in pieces rather than all at once.

The festival. The lake. Eren.

The kiss.

Then the forest.

Levi opened his eyes and stared at the ceiling for a moment.

His expression hardened.

Right.

By the time he left his quarters, he was washed, dressed and composed, every trace of exhaustion tucked neatly behind the familiar severity of Captain Levi.

The prisoners had been given the entire night to sober up, cool down and consider exactly how badly their evening had ended.

That was generous enough.

Levi headed toward the cells.

It was time to talk.

Eren

Connie was awake. The migraine was still pounding in his head, but his anxiety had taken over. He was beginning to grasp what was actually happening to them.

He paused when he heard footsteps approaching, and then Levi’s face appeared on the other side of the cell.

“Hey, Levi. Thanks for coming. Such an illustrious presence.”

He laughed briefly, though his body remained tense with anxiety. He noticed Levi’s expressionless face.

“Why are you always so serious? It doesn’t hurt to take life easy.”

Connie smiled and, with some difficulty due to his hangover, moved closer to the iron bars of his cell.

“You know, Levi… let’s be direct. I’m going to show you just how nice I am. Even after everything you’re doing to us, I’ll keep my voice down.”

He stared at Levi, his face practically pressed between the iron bars. Resting his face against them slightly so he could look directly into Levi’s eyes, he lowered his voice.

“I don’t give a fuck about what happens to me here, because I know that my fate—whether inside this place or outside of it—is going to be…”

He made the gesture of a knife slicing across his throat.

But then his eyes traveled over Levi before he continued in the same quiet tone.

“But… I was there.”

He laughed softly.

“What a lovely romance you two have. Hate and love, is that how it goes?”

He noticed the impatience in Levi’s expression.

“Anyway, I don’t care. What I do regret is not releasing that arrow…”

He sighed dramatically.

“My God, do I regret it.”

He watched Levi carefully.

“You’re confused? Oh, wait.”

Connie swallowed, his eyes fixed on Levi.

“I was aiming an arrow at you while the two of you were kissing.”

He paused.
