Chapter II

Eren

The village was dark, the sky full of stars, torches of fire spread along the path. From afar, several people carried their lanterns toward the large lake where later the lantern festival in the sky would take place. Everything seemed normal, calm—almost too calm.

The market stalls were there as well. The merchandise was scarce, yet everyone seemed very enthusiastic. Eren paused for a moment, thoughtful as he looked over the village.

— Hey… Eren? I’ve been talking for two hours here and you seem distracted.

Armin called his attention.

— Sorry, I’m going to pass by the stall just to check if everything’s okay with my mother. — he said, smiling.

— Sure! But after that straight to the tavern, okay? There’s dancing and you promised we’d have some drinks there. — Armin said as he walked away.

— Yes, I know. I’ll be there. — Eren nodded and walked along the row of stalls, observing and being greeted by some of the villagers while he smiled gently. A feeling of uncertainty crossed his mind for a moment. He kept walking until he reached his mother’s stall.

— My son, I’m so glad you’re here, look what I managed to get! — she said, extremely happy.

— Ginseng? But Mom, how did you get this? It’s rare around here these days, and we could sell it for a good price today. — He smiled, happy to see the smile on his mother’s face.

— Don’t ask me too much, I’ll explain later. But son, can you watch the stall while I help our neighbor with the rest of the preparations? It’ll be quick.

— Mom, don’t worry. I’ll take care of the stall for as long as necessary. — He smiled, watching her walk away while he arranged the ginseng on the table along with the other medicinal plants.

Levi

The castle gates opened just as the first lanterns began to glow in the distance.

Levi rode at the head of the small procession, dressed entirely in black — formal military coat fitted cleanly to his frame, gloves dark, boots polished to a dull shine. No ornament. No color. He did not dress for festivals. Only for duty.

Behind him, the royal carriage moved at a measured pace. Historia’s soft laughter drifted through the night air as she spoke to her husband. Reiner’s deeper voice answered, steady, reassuring.

Levi did not turn. He had heard their conversations countless times before.

Public morale, stability, appearances.

“The villages need to see us,” Historia was saying gently. “Especially after the storms.”

“And they will,” Reiner replied. “Tonight is meant to restore faith.”

Levi’s jaw tightened almost imperceptibly.

Faith was a fragile currency.

Torches lined the descending path toward the lake. From this distance, the festival lights looked like scattered stars fallen to earth. Lantern frames swayed lightly in the evening breeze, their paper glowing amber and gold.

Music carried faintly upward — pipes, drums, laughter. For a moment, it almost looked peaceful. Almost.

Hanji rode slightly behind Levi, far too cheerful for his liking.

“Try not to look like you’re marching to an execution,” she murmured.

“I’m not,” Levi replied flatly.

“You’re thinking it, though.”

He ignored her.

As they approached the outer edge of the village, more lanterns lifted into the air, drifting slowly upward. Small flames rising into darkness.

Levi’s eyes scanned the crowd automatically.

Exits. Clusters. Unusual spacing.

Hands that lingered near belts instead of cups.

He wasn’t here for the music but for the silence beneath it.

The procession slowed as villagers began to bow and murmur respectfully at the sight of the royal carriage.

Levi dismounted before the carriage came to a full stop, and did not wait for instruction. His boots touched the dirt, and his presence alone seemed to carve a small circle of space in the crowd.

Black against lantern light.

Unmoving. Watching.

Eren

— My God, the soldiers are so handsome. I think today we might find ourselves a husband. — Two women passed by the stall with their arms linked, covering their mouths as they laughed in an extremely exaggerated way.

Eren raised an eyebrow. He started to stand, but was surprised when Tony approached quietly.

— They’re here…

Eren’s expression changed completely. His eyes seemed to lose some of their shine, turning dull.

— Why do they insist on bothering us? As if it wasn’t enough that they don’t help and… — Eren sighed.

— Boss, they came with some additional soldiers, but it seems the Highness just wants to enjoy the festival. If I may give my opinion, I think tonight we should keep the festival and the village peaceful. They won’t cause any trouble…

Eren sighed again and uncrossed his arms.

— Yes… okay, you’re right, Tony. But keep an eye on the tavern area. No one is allowed to go in there. I’ll be there myself to have a few drinks and make sure everything is fine. Reinforce the security in the back. — he said promptly.

Tony nodded and left.

Eren placed one hand on the back of his neck and, out of curiosity, decided to step outside. In a quick glance, he saw a man dressed in black walking among the stalls. He didn’t look—didn’t bother to look. Instead, he turned away and pretended to adjust the stall’s plants, waiting for the man to pass.

He knew it was one of the soldiers.

Levi

Levi moved between the stalls without hurry.

Lantern light brushed over woven baskets, small bundles of herbs, jars of honey, rough bread loaves.

Scarcity disguised as celebration.

His gaze shifted once — briefly — to a young man adjusting a stall. The boy did not look at him.

Avoidance was rarely accidental. Levi continued walking. Eventually, he stopped.

Ginseng.

It lay carefully arranged beside sprigs of rosemary and basil, its pale root unmistakable even in low firelight.

He stepped closer.

“Where did you obtain this?” he asked calmly.

His voice was not loud. It didn’t need to be.

From this distance, the root was clearly mature — multiple growth scars along its neck. At least six years old. Possibly more.

Not village-grown.

He picked it up without asking permission, turning it slowly between his fingers.

“The soil here is too exposed,” he said almost absently. “Ginseng requires deep shade. Stable woodland. Undisturbed ground.”

His eyes lifted.

Green met grey for the first time.

“You’re selling something that doesn’t grow in your region.”

It was not an accusation. Not yet, just a simple observation.

The lantern light flickered between them.

Eren

Green eyes met gray ones for a brief moment, but Eren didn’t linger there. He stepped into the stall and began organizing the remaining merchandise on the table.

— Fortunately, we received some help from villages in the far north. I don’t know if you’re familiar with ginseng, but this plant is used to improve performance and increase energy. The neighboring village agreed we would need plenty of energy to rebuild our soil after the storm. They made a donation in exchange for other supplements. — It was everything Eren could think of while avoiding a real conversation with the man in front of him.

— Are you taking it or not? — he pressed impatiently.

At that moment, he briefly glanced at the man’s face, waiting for an answer.

In the distance, loud sounds began to rise. The festival had fully started.

Levi

Levi did not react to the explanation immediately.

He continued examining the root between his fingers, thumb brushing over the ridged scars along its neck.

Far north. He almost smiled.

“Interesting,” he said quietly.

The lantern light shifted as laughter rose from the lakeside. Music swelled. The festival had begun.

Levi set the ginseng back on the table — precisely where it had been.

“You say your village needs energy to rebuild,” he said, voice even.

His gaze lifted again.

“And yet you’re selling it.”

“If the soil is as damaged as you claim,” he continued, “and your people are exhausted from the storms… why trade away a root known to restore strength?”

His eyes lingered on Eren now.

Measured. Curious.

“Unless,” he added calmly, “the energy isn’t for the villagers.”

A lantern drifted upward behind them, casting a slow-moving glow across Levi’s black coat.

He had not introduced himself. He had not mentioned royalty. He looked like any soldier.

But his attention was sharper than that.

“Are you taking it or not?” Eren had asked.

Levi tilted his head slightly.

“I haven’t decided,” he replied.

He reached into his coat and placed a coin on the table — not enough for the full price.

“Convince me.”

Eren

Eren thought the explanation had been coherent enough. Why the hell would that man need explanations about a medicinal herb?

Eren softened his gaze and then let out a small laugh, crossing his arms.

— I’m usually not very good at convincing people to buy a simple herb. It’s not like I’m selling gold or diamonds here, right?

Eren spotted his mother approaching and then looked back at Levi.

— You could have a few drinks first and then come back to the stall. That way I can charge you triple and you won’t even question it. — he joked.

Carla caught the end of the conversation and laughed along, looking at Levi.

— Don’t mind him. I hope he isn’t bothering you. I hope you liked our stall. It’s quite simple, but unfortunately it’s all we have to offer at the moment. — she said with a gentle smile before turning to Eren.

— Son, the celebrations have already started at the tavern. They’re waiting for you. Take the young man who’s visiting the village so he can see a bit more of our culture. Would you like to go, young man? — she asked kindly.

Eren froze for a moment. His expression remained the same, but he felt as if at any second he wouldn’t be able to hide his discomfort anymore.

Levi

Levi watched the shift.

The laugh was too measured.

The arms crossed a little too deliberately.

Deflection.

He’d seen it before — men who filled silence with humor when they didn’t want questions answered.

But then the mother spoke.

Levi’s gaze shifted to her without changing expression.

Her hands were rough.

The stall was sparse.

Rosemary drying at the edges. Basil already wilting in the night air.

And the ginseng placed almost too carefully, as if it carried more weight than it should.

He didn’t miss the way the young man stiffened when she suggested the tavern.

Interesting.

“I’m not easily convinced,” Levi said calmly, eyes returning to Eren. “Especially by jokes.”

He then addressed Carla.

“It’s not a poor stall.”

It wasn’t praise. Just correction.

He reached into his coat again — this time placing enough coin on the table to cover the ginseng.

“I won’t be drinking,” he added lightly.

He picked up a small piece of parchment from inside his glove and, without ceremony, wrote a few lines.

Crushed barley.

Honey.

Dried mint.

Boiled together.

He slid the paper toward Carla.

“For fatigue,” he said. “More sustainable than ginseng. Easier to obtain.”

He looked once more at Eren.

“You should keep your strongest remedies. For yourselves.”

Lantern light flickered across his features again.

He did not introduce himself, for he never knew the boy's name.

He did not accept the tavern invitation.

He stepped back from the stall instead, already scanning the crowd again — but this time his attention lingered a fraction longer.

Not on the ginseng. On the boy.

Then he turned and disappeared into the moving glow of lanterns.

Eren

Eren said nothing, but his alert mode was fully awake. He watched every movement Levi made, though he avoided looking directly into his eyes. He wasn’t afraid—much less intimidated by whatever royal errand boy that man might be—but he felt something different, a faint discomfort. Still, he told himself that nothing would ruin his night.

He seemed relieved when the other man finally left, though he remained attentive.

Eren also walked away and headed toward the tavern.

When he arrived, he looked around. The tavern was packed—mugs of beer, plenty of food, people dancing, lively music filling the room.

— Finally, Eren… what happened? — Armin asked as Eren sat down at the counter on the bar side.

— Nothing… just a little annoyed. — Eren replied, signaling to Connie.

Connie understood immediately and brought him a drink.

— C’mon, Eren, not today… — Armin said, holding his friend’s face. — Seriously… tonight we’re just going to have fun and—

Armin stopped mid-sentence when he heard one of the songs they used to dance to as children. It was an upbeat and famous song in the village. Soon the entire tavern began singing along, clapping their hands, and shouting a loud, deep “Hey!” with the rhythm.

Eren smiled and started singing along too, looking at Armin, who pulled him toward the center of the tavern. The two of them began dancing with their drinks still in their hands.

The tavern door didn’t stop opening and closing. Every second new people entered, bringing with them the wind, the darkness of the night, and the flickering light of the torches outside.

For some reason, even though he was enjoying himself, Eren kept his eyes drifting back to the door. Eventually he decided he was just being paranoid and returned to the celebration.

This time, he pulled Sasha—another childhood friend and one of the tavern’s dancers—into the dance.

Levi

Levi did not return to the stall.

He continued along the perimeter of the festival, boots steady against packed earth. Lantern light brushed against his coat, but never softened it.

The royal couple had already taken position near the lake. Applause rose as Historia greeted the villagers warmly, her voice carrying gentle reassurance. Reiner stood beside her, composed, solid.

Levi remained several steps behind, watching, listening.

He caught small details others would ignore — a group dispersing too quickly when guards passed, a merchant whispering mid-laughter, a hand lingering too long near a concealed blade before releasing it.

Nothing actionable.

Hanji appeared at his side without warning.

“Should we—,” she said lightly.

“No. I’m working.”

“You’ve been working for three hours.”

He didn’t respond.

She followed his gaze toward the tavern, where laughter spilled loudly through the open door.

“Relax,” she said. “If something were going to explode tonight, you’d feel it already.”

He did feel something. He just couldn’t name it.

Hanji nudged his arm.

“One drink. In the corner. You can keep brooding if it makes you happy.”

“I don’t brood.”

“You radiate it.”

Levi exhaled quietly.

“Five minutes.”

“That’s the spirit.”

They approached the tavern.

The door opened as someone stumbled out laughing, nearly colliding with them. Music thundered inside — drums, clapping, voices rising together in familiar rhythm.

Levi stepped in last.

The warmth hit immediately. Beer, smoke, sweat, woodfire. Noise. Too much of it.

He scanned automatically.

And there — movement in the center of the room.

The same green eyes from earlier, brighter now in the firelight, laughing, spinning into a dance with friends. Unrestrained. Alive in a way that didn’t match the guarded stillness at the stall.

Levi didn’t stare, but he didn’t look away either.

“Corner,” he muttered.

Hanji grinned and led the way to a shadowed table near the wall.

Two drinks arrived without them asking.

Levi didn’t touch his immediately. His gaze drifted once more toward the center of the tavern.

Assessment.

The boy was watching the door even while he danced, Levi noticed.

Definitely interesting.

He finally took a small sip.

And kept watching.

Eren

Eren noticed something, though he perhaps pretended not to. He continued dancing and laughing with his friends until he finally glanced at the door and saw the young man. A wide smile spread across his face.

— Jean! We were waiting for you! — Armin shouted as the young man made his way toward them, pausing briefly to give Eren a hug.

Eren returned it with a light slap on his back.

They talked for a while. Jean had temporarily moved to another village to take care of his parents, although he had spent a good part of his life in Ashvale. They kept chatting, laughing, catching up.

Eren carefully observed the surroundings. He didn’t look at the soldier, but he knew he was there—and that someone else was with him.

Eren quickly scanned again and, a few meters away from the soldier, he saw a familiar presence: Patrick, extremely drunk, holding one of the dancers in his arms while running his hand over her and dropping a few coins.

Eren watched the scene briefly but decided to stay cautious.

He grabbed his beer and a shot of vodka in his hand.

Passing by Levi, he paused.

— Curious? Decided to visit us here, that’s nice. — he smiled, greeting the woman next to him, unsure if she was the other man’s girlfriend or just an acquaintance.

— This is for you, as a welcome to the village. Shots!

Levi

Levi had noticed the drunk man long before the boy approached their table.

The grip was possessive. The laughter too loud. Coins exchanged as if they excused everything.

The dancer’s smile was practiced.

When the boy stopped in front of them with beer in one hand and clear liquor in the other, Levi didn’t react immediately. He studied him first — the posture, the way his attention moved across the room without appearing to.

“Curious? Decided to visit us here, that’s nice.”

Levi let his gaze lift slowly.

“This is for you, as a welcome to the village. Shots.”

Beside him, Hanji leaned forward, already amused.

“Oh, now that’s—”

“No.”

Hanji leaned back with an exaggerated sigh.

Levi’s eyes returned to the young man.

“We don't hold our liquor well,” he pretexted calmly.

The music swelled again as another round of cheers erupted somewhere behind them. Patrick’s laughter cut through it — heavier now, rougher. The dancer shifted in his arms, her hand pressing against his chest with more insistence than affection.

Levi’s attention drifted there briefly.

Then he leaned slightly toward Hanji, speaking low enough not to draw attention.

“If he forgets himself, you move.”

Her expression changed at once. She nodded.

Levi looked back at the boy.

“You’re generous,” he said evenly. “But you haven’t stopped watching the door since you walked in.”

He didn’t accuse him.

He didn’t smile.

He simply observed.

“And that doesn’t look like someone here only for celebration.”

Eren

Eren kept his observation; indeed, that guy was something else. Cautious, alert, and extremely suspicious.

Eren subtly pulled out a chair and sat down for a moment between the soldiers.

— Alright, I’m not going to waste this. — He smiled and grabbed the shot, tipping it back and drinking it all.

A few meters away, Patrick’s insistent voice could be heard.

Eren was ready to question why they were visiting the village, but he was taken aback by the man’s perception.

— Was I being that obvious? My Lord… — he laughed quietly. — Okay, I was indeed waiting for that guy over there. — He pointed to Jean, who was laughing and chatting with the others.

He didn’t give any explanation; he didn’t need to. But from the look, it might have been easy to understand that perhaps Jean had a stronger bond with Eren or maybe not.

And at that moment, Patrick’s voice and immediate uproar boiled Eren’s blood.

He tried his best not to show it, but he noticed that Levi had taken note.

— Don’t worry, — he leaned close to Levi’s ear and whispered — A village schizophrenic. Has problems with alcohol and freaks out every time.

He pulled back, returning to his normal posture.

— But tell me, to what does the village owe the honor of your visit? Which village do you belong to?

Levi

Levi did not move when the boy leaned close enough for breath to touch his ear.

“A village schizophrenic,” he murmured. “Has problems with alcohol and freaks out every time.”

Levi’s eyes did not leave the room. Across the tavern, Patrick’s voice had risen again — sharper now, angry. A chair scraped loudly against the floor.

Hanji excused herself.

She didn’t wait for further instruction. She slipped through the crowd with disarming ease, smile bright, posture relaxed — until it wasn’t.

Levi remained seated.

“You’re generous,” he said calmly, “in your descriptions.”

He turned his head slightly, finally giving Eren his full attention.

Patrick’s shouting cut off abruptly somewhere behind them.

Levi continued, as if nothing had happened.

“You ask which village I belong to.”

His gaze held steady.

“You were watching the procession when we arrived. You saw who stepped out of the carriage.”

The lantern light flickered across his features, but his expression did not shift.

“So I doubt you need me to explain where I’m from.”

A quiet beat passed between them.

“As for your friend,” he added, glancing once toward the man Eren had pointed out earlier, “if that had been the reason you were watching the door, you would have looked relieved when he entered.”

His eyes returned to Eren.

“You didn’t.”

He took a slow sip from his glass.

“Careful men don’t ask questions they already know the answers to.”

He set the drink down.

“My colleague seems occupied.”

He stood without hurry.

“Enjoy your festival.”
