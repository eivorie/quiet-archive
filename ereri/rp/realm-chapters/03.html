Chapter III

Levi

The royal chamber was warm despite the late hour. Several candles still burned low along the stone walls, their light reflecting faintly against polished metal and silk drapery. Reiner stood near the window overlooking the lake, hands clasped behind his back, watching the last remnants of lantern debris drift toward the shore.

When Levi entered, he did not turn immediately.

“She’s more tired than she admits,” Reiner said at last.

Levi closed the door behind him and waited.

“Fatigue is expected,” he replied evenly. “Her condition is stable.”

Reiner turned then, the weight of private concern visible in the set of his jaw.

“I don’t want stable,” he said. “I want certainty.”

There was no anger in his voice. Only fear carefully restrained.

“I want additional provisions brought to the castle. More medicinal herbs. Higher-grade grains. Strengthening tonics. Anything that reduces risk.”

Levi considered him before answering.

“The current stores are sufficient.”

“For now,” Reiner replied. “But I will not gamble with her health.”

Levi stepped closer to the table between them.

“Increasing requisitions now will be noticed,” he said calmly. “The outer villages are already adjusting to reduced yields from the storm. Further withdrawals will widen that strain.”

Reiner’s gaze sharpened. “Are you suggesting the Queen’s pregnancy should be secondary to village discomfort?”

“I am suggesting,” Levi answered without raising his voice, “that the realm cannot afford to associate her pregnancy with scarcity.”

Silence settled between them.

“If supplies begin to thin visibly after this announcement, resentment will not be directed at the weather.”

Reiner exhaled slowly.

“What would you propose instead?”

Levi did not hesitate.

“Invite representatives from each village. One or two at a time. Let them train here under supervision.”

“In medicine?”

“In preservation. Substitution methods. Efficient preparation using limited ingredients. There are restorative techniques that require no rare crops at all.”

He held the king’s gaze steadily.

“If they return equipped to produce more with less, pressure eases. The castle is seen sharing knowledge rather than extracting resources.”

Reiner remained quiet for several seconds.

“And Historia?” he asked at last.

“She will not lack,” Levi replied. “But neither should the rest of the realm.”

Reiner nodded once.

“Draft the structure,” he said. “We will review it formally.”

Levi inclined his head and withdrew.

Hanji was waiting just beyond the chamber doors, leaning casually against the wall.

“Well?” she asked.

“He wants increased provisions brought in,” Levi said as they began walking down the corridor.

Hanji’s expression shifted, though she masked it quickly.

“Of course he does.”

Levi’s steps remained measured.

“At the festival,” he said, “many of the stalls were stretched thin. Some were decorative more than stocked.”

“You noticed that,” she replied quietly.

“They are managing with less,” Levi continued. “If we take more now, it will not go unnoticed.”

Hanji studied him for a moment.

“So you suggested training.”

“Yes.”

She gave a faint smile.

“You don’t usually volunteer to educate half the countryside.”

“It isn’t education,” Levi replied. “It’s maintenance. And necessity.”

Hanji did not miss the weight beneath that word.

“You’re thinking ahead.”

“I prefer to.”

She walked beside him in silence for a few steps.

“And if one of those villages sends someone… interesting?” she added lightly.

Levi did not respond, which, in itself, was answer enough.
