Chapter VIII

Levi

By the time Levi returned to his quarters, Connie's voice had become considerably more irritating in memory than it had been through the bars.

"I was aiming an arrow at you while the two of you were kissing."

Levi closed the door behind him and stood there for a moment.

Then he removed his jacket, folded it over the back of a chair and sat down.

Connie's story explained the knife.

That was all it did.

It didn't explain why Eren had kissed him rather than simply warning him. It didn't explain why he'd apologized afterward. More importantly, it came from a man who had been drunk enough to ride through a forest with a bow in his hand and stupid enough to draw it against a royal patrol.

Connie was Eren's companion. He had every reason to lie for him.

Levi wasn't interested in deciding which version he preferred.

He wanted facts.

A short while later, he returned to the lower levels of the castle. He stopped one of the guards outside the cells and gave the order without looking toward the corridor where Eren had spent the night.

— Bring Jaeger to the interrogation room. Alone.

The guard nodded.

Levi paused.

— And bring me the knife you confiscated from him.

Several minutes later, Levi was already waiting.

The room was deliberately plain: stone walls, a narrow table and two chairs facing one another. Eren's knife lay horizontally on the wood in front of Levi.

When the door finally opened, Levi didn't rise.

His eyes lifted to Eren.

Cold. Steady. Impossible to read.

The guard brought him inside.

— Remove the restraints.

Once it was done, Levi waited until the guard had left and the door had closed behind him.

Only then did he gesture toward the empty chair.

— Sit.

No accusation followed. No mention of the lake, the kiss, Connie or the forest.

Levi simply watched him take his place.

Then he pushed the knife across the table until it stopped in front of Eren.

— Start talking.

Eren

Eren was caught off guard by one of the guards unlocking the cell, who grabbed him by the arm. He gave no warning, simply telling Eren that he needed to follow him. At first, Eren hesitated, but deep down, he was already fully aware of where he was being taken.

Eren studied the path. He said nothing, simply following as the guard held his wrist firmly.

He entered the room. Eren’s eyes didn’t need to meet his; he could see his silhouette from the corner of his eye. Not once did Eren look at Levi. He preferred to keep his gaze on the knife positioned on the table.

Eren sat down, slowly moving his wrists. The relief was palpable. They felt a little sore, nothing that made much of a difference.

“I have absolutely nothing to say.”

His eyes remained fixed on the knife resting on the table.

“Could you tell me how my companions are doing? Armin... I need to know how he is.”

Levi

Levi watched him for a moment.

Nothing in his expression changed.

— Fine.

He reached for the knife, drew it back across the table and stood.

No threat. No attempt to persuade him. No second question.

Levi crossed to the door and opened it.

— Guard.

The soldier stationed outside appeared almost immediately.

— Take him back to his cell.

Only then did Levi's eyes return to Eren. They were as cold and impersonal as they had been in the forest.

— You said you had absolutely nothing to say. I'm not going to waste my time convincing you otherwise.

He stepped aside to let the guard enter.

As for Armin, Levi gave him nothing.

No reassurance. No information. Not even confirmation that he was still somewhere below the castle.

Eren had been offered a conversation.

He'd declined it.

— Restrain him and take him back.

Eren

Eren wasn’t taken by surprise. He simply felt a sensation he couldn’t quite explain. He hadn’t expected a gentle gesture from Levi, but deep down, he would have preferred Levi to act with his hands rather than with complete indifference.

Eren said nothing, nor did he look at Levi at any point. He simply offered a small smile, not ironic, not amused, but incredulous.

Eren waited until the guard approached him, and as he chained his hands to take him back to his cell, Eren spoke calmly, in a low tone.

“You’re so caught up in your own suspicions that you haven’t even realized one of your companions hasn’t returned to the castle. You should be worried.”

He said this as the guard abruptly grabbed him by the arm to make him stand.

* * * * *
Eren

Before Eren could leave, Tony promptly stopped him.

“Boss, this is too risky. I don’t think you should take the lead on this.”

Eren let out a heavy sigh, still agitated by Connie’s irresponsible behavior.

“I understand, but there’s no arguing about this. I need to go with them. It’s more important than you think. I wish this were only about something thoughtless Connie did,” he said, mounting his horse.

“Boss, before you go, I need a name. I need a name in case something goes wrong… You know, it’s the plan.”

Eren hesitated, falling silent for a second. He adjusted the equipment on his clothes. He quickly recalled her getting embarrassed by Levi’s help, followed by their endless conversation. It was unthinkable, but Eren didn’t hesitate.

“Petra.”

Levi

Levi's eyes sharpened.

That was the first visible reaction Eren had managed to draw from him.

Not fear.

Calculation.

— Stop.

The guard halted before reaching the door.

Levi looked at him rather than Eren.

— Have the morning watch verify every soldier who returned from Ashvale last night. Names, posts, everything. If anyone is unaccounted for, I want to know immediately.

— Yes, Captain.

The guard hesitated, waiting.

Levi's gaze finally shifted back to Eren.

So he did have something to say.

He had simply decided that information concerning one of Levi's people didn't qualify until it could be used on his way out of the room.

Whatever faint uncertainty Connie's story had introduced that morning disappeared beneath something colder.

— Take him back.

The guard tightened his hold on Eren's arm.

Levi didn't stop him again.

— And Jaeger?

His voice remained quiet.

— If one of my people is missing and you knew about it before you were arrested, you'd better spend the walk back deciding whether you still have "absolutely nothing to say."

He turned away.

— Go.
