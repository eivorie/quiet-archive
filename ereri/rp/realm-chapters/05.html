Chapter V

Eren

They finally made it to the tavern. Eren held Levi firmly in his arms as they slipped through a back entrance that had no access to the main area of the tavern, which was already closed for the night.

Eren carefully positioned Levi while Armin and Jean tied his arms down as a precaution. Eren watched closely, making sure the restraints weren’t hurting him, and then dismissed Armin and Jean despite both of them insisting that it wasn’t a good idea. Eren assured them that everything was under control and that Tony would be there to help.

Only the crackling of the fireplace echoed through the hideout. Eren sat in his chair and picked up a few papers, trying to focus, but his eyes inevitably drifted toward Levi. His head had fallen forward. Eren sighed and hesitated for a moment before getting up and walking over to him.

The mask covered Eren’s entire face, leaving Levi’s features visible. Eren took hold of a few strands of Levi’s hair and gently pulled them back, lifting his head. He was still unconscious. Eren checked his pulse; everything was under control.

For a moment, Eren studied Levi’s face more carefully. His thumb brushed over Levi’s cheek in an involuntary gesture until his hand moved to the back of Levi’s neck. He wanted to make sure there was nothing left behind from the blow. Nothing. No wound.

Eren leaned a little closer, looking at Levi and feeling his breath against his own, until the moment was completely interrupted by the door slamming open. Eren startled and immediately let go of Levi.

“Boss, the doctor is here,” Tony said.

Eren ran a hand over the back of his neck and returned to his chair, slightly restless.

“Come in.”

The older man entered and began examining Levi. Eren watched everything from a distance, making sure every step was carried out properly. After a few minutes, the man stood and walked toward the door, stopping in front of Eren.

“Everything is fine. His pulse is stable, and his heart rate is a little elevated because of the sudden dose, but nothing that puts him at risk. I can’t give you an accurate estimate of how long he’ll remain unconscious, but I don’t believe it’ll be for very long.”

Eren nodded.

“Don’t you think I should take him back now?” Tony asked.

“No. I’m going to wait until he wakes up,” Eren said. “You can leave.”

Levi

Consciousness returned in fragments. Heat first. The faint crackle of a fire somewhere nearby. Then the dull weight behind Levi's eyes and an unpleasant dryness at the back of his throat.

Voices.

Too distant to distinguish at first.

He didn't move.

Years of training overruled instinct before his eyes could even open.

Assess first.

His head was lowered. He was sitting rather than lying down. His boots touched solid flooring. No wind. No smell of wet earth.

Indoors.

Then he felt the pressure around his wrists.

Restraints.

Levi's thoughts cleared considerably faster than his body did.

Well.

That was a problem.

He tested one wrist with a movement so small it wouldn't be visible from across the room.

Tied securely. Not painfully. That detail irritated him more than it reassured him.

Someone had taken care not to hurt him. How considerate.

A door opened somewhere nearby.

Levi caught only the end of an exchange before footsteps retreated and the door closed again.

Silence followed.

Not complete silence. Someone else was still in the room.

Levi remained motionless, breathing evenly as memory began assembling itself.

The forest. The riders. Eren behind him. An arrow. The path.

Then—

His jaw tightened. Pieces were missing. Levi hated missing pieces. But there were enough. Far too many, actually.

The stolen crops. The conveniently failed archery. The instincts that appeared whenever Eren stopped having time to think. His knowledge of the forest. His insistence that Levi shouldn't go searching that night.

And Levi, despite noticing every damned inconsistency, had still taken him along.

Because he'd trusted him. Not completely. But enough.

That distinction suddenly felt pathetic.

His fingers curled once against the restraints.

Anger came quietly.

Not hot. Not reckless. It settled somewhere beneath his ribs, cold and precise.

He'd been played.

The realization should have simplified everything.

It didn't.

Because whoever had brought him here had also made sure the ropes weren't cutting into his wrists.

Someone had apparently examined him.

And through the lingering haze in his head, Levi found himself remembering the last person he'd seen before everything disappeared.

Eren.

Of course it was fucking Eren.

Levi finally opened his eyes, slowly. The fire blurred before sharpening into focus. Wooden walls. Papers. A room he didn't recognize.

And then him.

Levi's gaze found Eren and stayed there.

No relief crossed his face. No surprise either. He simply looked at him. For several seconds, he said nothing. It gave him time to notice everything—the mask, the posture, the distance Eren had deliberately placed between them.

Levi leaned back as far as the restraints allowed.

“You know...”

His voice came out rougher than usual. He cleared his throat once.

“When you offered me seven days in the forest, this isn't quite what I thought you had in mind.”

The joke landed without warmth.

His eyes dropped briefly to his bound arms before returning to the masked figure.

“Though I suppose I should've asked for details.”

He tested the restraints openly this time.

Then stopped.

There was no point wasting strength. Not yet.

More importantly, Levi wanted answers before whoever had arranged this decided what happened next.

His gaze remained fixed on Eren.

Something about the silence between them felt worse than the ropes.

Only hours ago, that same man had brushed dirt from Levi's cheek as though it were the most natural thing in the world.

Levi had let him.

That memory pissed him off more than being captured did.

Still, when he spoke again, his voice remained controlled.

“So.”

A small pause.

“Are you going to take that thing off and explain why I'm tied to a chair...”

His eyes hardened.

“...or are we going to keep pretending I don't know who's standing in front of me?”

Eren

Eren swallowed hard. The room was completely dark, and only the flickering firelight cast its glow over the two of them. Eren was surprised by how quickly Levi had put the pieces together, but at the same time, he already knew the risk he was taking.

Eren grabbed one of his bottles and poured himself a drink, downing the shot in one go. He seemed to be sweating slightly, then calmly rolled up his sleeves, still remaining where he was as he watched Levi from a distance.

He turned around and stood facing his desk, leaning against it as he crossed his arms.

“You need to trust me…” was all he said.

Levi

For a second, Levi genuinely wondered whether whatever they'd given him was still affecting his hearing.

Then a short, humorless laugh escaped him.

“Trust you.”

He repeated it slowly, as though testing whether the words became any less ridiculous the second time. They didn't.

Levi shifted against the chair, the restraints immediately reminding him of the situation.

“That's an interesting choice of words coming from the man who had me drugged and tied to a fucking chair.”

His gaze followed Eren across the room. The drink. The rolled sleeves. The refusal to come any closer.

Nervous? Good. At least Eren understood that something had changed.

“What exactly is the plan here, Jaeger? You keep the mask on, refuse to explain anything, and I suddenly decide this all looks trustworthy?”

He tilted his head slightly.

“Because if that's your strategy, your bow isn't the only thing that needs work.”

The bite came naturally, but Levi felt none of the satisfaction it normally brought. Instead, his eyes dropped briefly to the floor.

Trust me.

That was the problem. Levi had. Against his better judgment, perhaps, but he had.

He'd noticed the inconsistencies. He'd questioned Eren's convenient incompetence. He'd known something wasn't adding up, and still, when the situation had turned dangerous, he'd put Eren beside him.

He'd listened when Eren gave directions, trusted the paths he chose. Trusted him at his back.

And when Eren had grabbed his hand and asked him not to enter the forest that night, Levi had interpreted it as concern before suspicion.

Apparently that had been his mistake.

His eyes returned to Eren.

“I did trust you.”

The sarcasm disappeared entirely. Levi let the silence sit between them before continuing.

“Enough to take you with me tonight. Enough to put you behind my back while someone was firing arrows at us. Enough that when you told me left, I went left without wasting time wondering whether you were sending me into a trap.”

His jaw tightened.

“So don't stand there and ask me for something you already had.”

For the first time since waking, Levi pulled once against the restraints—not because he expected them to give, but because the frustration needed somewhere to go.

“If you wanted me dead, I'd probably be dead. If you wanted me injured, you wouldn't have brought someone here to check me over.”

His eyes flicked briefly toward the door.

He hadn't heard enough to know exactly who had been there, but he knew an examination when he woke from one.

“So I'm still sitting here instead of trying to break your damn nose.”

“Consider that the last piece of trust you've got left.”

Levi leaned back again.

“Use it.”

His gaze settled on the mask.

“Take that off. Untie me.”

Then directly on the eyes behind it.

“And tell me the truth, Eren.”

Eren

Eren remained in the same position. The shadows of the hideout concealed most of his expression, but something had changed in his eyes. He stayed silent, watching Levi, refusing to move or remove the mask from his face.

Eren’s voice was even.

“I can’t explain anything to you right now. I need you to trust me.”

He sighed softly.

“Your intelligence and your ability to notice things are quite impressive, but don’t fool yourself into thinking you know everything.”

He rested both fists against the desk.

Levi

Levi stared at him.

For several seconds, nothing moved between them except the firelight.

Then his eyebrows lifted slightly.

“You're serious.”

It wasn't a question. Another quiet laugh escaped him, but this one held more disbelief than amusement.

“So let me understand this correctly. You won't explain anything. You won't take off the mask. You won't untie me. But you'd like me to trust you while reminding me that I don't know enough to understand why you've done any of it.”

His head tipped back against the chair.

“Fucking brilliant.”

Eren's comment about his intelligence didn't soften anything. If anything, it irritated him further.

“I never said I knew everything. That's why I'm asking you.”

Levi shifted his wrists slightly, the ropes scraping against his gloves.

“And apparently your answer is 'nothing'.”

There was a difference between keeping a secret and demanding blind obedience. Eren seemed determined to find it by stepping directly over the line.

Levi studied him again. The fists against the desk. The distance. The measured voice that didn't quite match the tension in his body.

Whatever Eren was protecting, it mattered enough for him to risk this. That was information in itself. But Levi wasn't going to reward him for it.

“You want me to trust that you have your reasons? Fine. I'm willing to believe you have reasons. That doesn't make them good ones.”

His voice had lost most of its anger now. What remained was colder.

“And trust isn't some fucking command you give when explaining yourself becomes inconvenient.”

Levi's eyes dropped deliberately toward Eren's clenched hands before returning to the mask.

“You knew enough about me to know I'd notice something eventually. You still chose this.”

He pulled once against the restraints, just enough to make the point.

“So whatever happens next is yours to deal with.”

Then Levi leaned forward as much as the chair allowed.

“I'm not asking you to tell me everything.”

That concession cost him something, but it was deliberate.

“Tell me why I'm here. Tell me whether those missing soldiers are alive. Tell me whether the people shooting arrows at us were yours.”

His expression hardened.

“Give me one actual reason to believe I'm not sitting in the middle of something I should be stopping. You keep asking me to trust you, Eren.”

His jaw tightened.

“Give me something to trust.”

His eyes remained fixed on Eren.

“Or you won't give me a choice.”

The silence stretched for another second.

Then Levi's expression suddenly tightened.

A sharp breath caught between his teeth as his body folded forward slightly against the restraints, his face twisting as though a sudden pain had struck somewhere beneath his ribs.

“Fuck...”

His head dropped, eyes squeezing shut for a brief moment.

Eren

Eren listened carefully to Levi, and at times he found himself unable to meet his eyes. He didn’t quite know how to describe the feeling, but there was a knot in his throat. A mixture of wanting to trust Levi and tell him everything, but also a mixture of fear of all the consequences it could bring—not only for himself, but for his soldiers, the village, and even Levi.

Eren heard a murmur, the sounds of horses approaching. He immediately went on alert and opened one of his drawers, taking one of his knives and hiding it in his pocket.

“Damn it…” he muttered, on high alert as he glanced quickly at Levi. He approached him and crouched down in front of him, then removed his mask, allowing Levi to see his face and, most importantly, look him in the eyes.

“Levi, listen. I need you to trust me, please…” The footsteps were getting closer, and the voices louder. “Pretend you’re unconscious.”

Eren didn’t have time to hear any response from Levi. He turned around, putting his mask back on just as the door was practically kicked down.

Tony was thrown onto the floor, a strip of cloth tied over his mouth, preventing him from speaking or giving Eren the warning he would have given him in a situation like this.

Three hooded men entered the hideout. Eren already knew who they were.

One of the men grabbed Eren by the neck and slammed him against the wall.

“Who the hell do you think you are, boy?” The man tightened his grip around Eren’s neck. “Was it part of the plan to bring this soldier here? … NO, IT WASNT.” The man answered himself, choking Eren. “You know, my young man, that if this soldier gives your name to the royal family tomorrow morning, you’ll have your head cut off in a spectacle for the entire village to watch?”

Eren tried to break free, but one of the other men stepped forward and restrained his arms.

“Boss, the soldier appears to be unconscious,” the other hooded man said, causing Eren’s eyes to close in relief for a moment, despite his restricted breathing.

“And so what if the soldier is unconscious? Do you know who that soldier is? Right now, the entire royal family is looking for him, and this imbecile is keeping him here with some damn plan that wasn’t even listed.”

Levi

Levi didn't move when the door crashed open. He didn't react when Tony hit the floor, either, or when Eren was driven against the wall hard enough for the impact to echo through the room. He simply let his head hang forward, kept his breathing shallow and did exactly what Eren had asked of him.

For once, it proved useful. In less than a minute, these men had given Levi more information than Eren had managed since he'd woken up. Bringing him here hadn't been part of their plan. The royal family was already searching for him. More importantly, Eren apparently understood perfectly well what would happen if Levi returned to the castle and gave them his name. Execution. Publicly, if the bastard currently crushing his throat was to be believed.

So that was what Eren had been so afraid of.

It didn't explain everything. It certainly didn't excuse everything. But it shifted enough pieces around that Levi was willing to keep listening.

At least until the man's grip tightened.

Levi heard the change in Eren's breathing before he saw anything. Another man caught his arms, preventing him from fighting back properly, and whatever patience Levi had left began thinning rapidly. He gave them another few seconds, partly because interfering too soon would waste the opportunity Eren had just handed him, partly because Eren had specifically asked him to stay down.

By the time the voices finally moved away and the room fell quiet again, Levi's patience was gone.

He lifted his head.

His eyes went to Eren first.

Only briefly.

He took in the way he stood, the movement of his chest, the absence of anything that suggested the bastard had suffered more than a bruised throat and a thoroughly deserved scare. Alive. Breathing. Capable of standing.

Good enough.

Levi looked down at the ropes around his wrists.

“I've wasted enough time here.”

There was no anger in his voice anymore. That had burned itself into something considerably more useful.

His soldiers were still missing. That mattered more than whatever explanation Eren was apparently determined not to give him.

Levi raised his eyes again.

“Do you have the other two?”

His voice was flat, stripped of everything except the question itself.

“The soldiers who disappeared from the patrol. Are they here?”

He watched Eren carefully, waiting for the answer.

“If your people took them, I want them back.”

Then Levi lowered his gaze to his wrists.

He turned one hand slightly, working his thumb against a knot he had already understood some time ago. A pull loosened the first loop. He slipped his hand through it, reached across, and made short work of the other.

The ropes fell quietly onto the floor.

Levi rubbed one wrist before getting to his feet.

He could have done that earlier.

Much earlier.

He offered no explanation for why he hadn't.

“Alive, preferably. I've had enough surprises for one night.”

His gaze briefly passed over Tony before returning to Eren.

“Get me my men, Jaeger.”

Eren

Eren’s breathing slowly began to steady as he watched Levi easily free himself from the ropes. Eren said nothing, simply observing Levi’s skill in silence, though he didn’t force himself to think too much about it. He turned around and helped Tony, cutting through his ropes.

“Are you okay?” Eren asked, making sure. “Go home… that’s enough for today.”

“But, boss, I can’t leave you here with—”

Eren cut him off. “It’s fine… They won’t come back here tonight, and I believe Levi is already on his way out.” Eren glanced quickly at Levi before looking back at Tony and nodding.

Tony seemed hesitant and took a while to leave, but he looked at Eren again, and Eren nodded once more.

Eren opened his drawers and took out a piece of cotton, then grabbed a mixture of arnica he had made himself from one of the cabinets. He sat down at his desk while applying the mixture to the cotton.

“I don’t know what happened to the soldiers,” he said as he brought the cotton to his ribs, slowly applying it and narrowing his eyes slightly at the sting.

Levi

Levi watched Tony leave without trying to stop him. The door closed behind the man, leaving the two of them alone again with the fire and considerably more questions than Levi had any intention of asking tonight.

Eren's answer was the only one that mattered.

“I don't know what happened to the soldiers.”

Levi's jaw tightened.

“Then we're done here.”

He reached for the equipment that had been taken from him, checking each piece methodically before putting it back where it belonged. His movements remained calm, but whatever patience he'd shown earlier was gone.

From the corner of his eye, he saw Eren press the cotton against his ribs.

Levi looked. Of course he did. The healer in him registered the location automatically, the way Eren's body tightened when the arnica touched the bruised area, the controlled breath that followed. Nothing immediately alarming. Painful, certainly. Probably ugly by morning.

For one stupid second, Levi's attention lingered.

A few hours ago, he might have crossed the room without thinking about it. Taken the cotton from Eren's hand, checked the injury properly and told him he was doing it wrong even if he wasn't.

Now, Levi stayed where he was. Eren knew how to treat a bruise. He could manage.

Levi secured the last strap and finally looked at him properly.

“If you really don't know where they are, I'll find them myself.”

His gaze dropped once toward Eren's ribs before returning to his face.

“And put some ice on that when you're done. Arnica isn't going to do much for the swelling.”

Apparently professional irritation survived betrayal.

Levi turned toward the exit, then stopped with his hand near the door.

“One more thing.”

He looked back.

“Those men were afraid I'd give your name to the Crown. I heard them.”

Nothing else. No promise to protect Eren, but more importantly, no threat to turn him in either.

Levi left the meaning of that exactly where it was, and left without another glance.

Eren

Eren listened to Levi’s movements as he applied the medicine to himself, wincing with each breath as he felt the scar burning. Eren kept his gaze fixed on his own body as he spoke.

“I don’t know what happened to them, but you shouldn’t be wandering around in the middle of the night. They’re probably at the castle waiting for the morning banquet while you’re the one in danger.”

Levi ignored what he said and calmly continued gathering his things until he turned toward Eren and mentioned the ice. That made Eren involuntarily lift his gaze, and he saw Levi’s eyes wandering between his ribs and his own eyes. Eren followed Levi with his gaze until he saw him stop at the door.

The last sentence struck Eren in a certain way, leaving him standing there, staring at the fireplace as he found himself surrounded by the sudden silence of the tavern.

Levi

Levi stepped out into the night without looking back.

The cold air hit harder after the warmth of the hideout, clearing what little remained of the fog in his head. He adjusted his cloak, found his bearings, and started toward the castle.

Eren's warning followed him anyway.

You shouldn't be wandering around in the middle of the night.

Levi almost scoffed aloud.

That was rich coming from the man whose associates had fired arrows at him in the middle of the night before someone drugged him and tied him to a chair.

Whether Eren had ordered any of it was another question entirely.

That was the problem.

Levi no longer knew where Eren's involvement began and ended.

He believed bringing him to the hideout hadn't been planned. The men tonight had made that perfectly clear. He believed Eren had been genuinely afraid when they'd threatened what would happen if Levi revealed his identity. He even believed, irritatingly enough, that Eren had been concerned about him.

None of that meant Eren hadn't lied.

And until Levi knew what had happened to the missing soldiers, he wasn't prepared to take a single thing Jaeger said at face value.

The journey back gave him too much time to think.

The failed shots with the bow. Eren's familiarity with the forest. The thefts from royal lands. The masked men. Tony calling him boss. The way Eren had asked him not to enter the forest before any of this had happened.

Levi had noticed every warning sign.

He'd simply chosen, repeatedly, to give Eren the benefit of the doubt.

That particular privilege was gone.

By the time the castle came into view, dawn wasn't far away. Activity near the gates immediately told him the search hadn't stopped in his absence. Guards moved through the courtyard carrying lanterns, horses stood saddled near the entrance, and several familiar voices cut through the commotion.

“Levi!”

Hanji crossed the courtyard toward him so quickly he barely had time to brace himself.

“Where the hell have you been? We've had people looking everywhere for you.”

“Nice to see you too.”

Hanji ignored that entirely, looking him over with increasingly obvious suspicion.

“Are you hurt?”

“No.”

“You look terrible.”

“Thanks.”

“And you've got marks around your wrists.”

Levi pulled his gloves farther up.

“Anything else you'd like to inspect?”

Hanji stared at him for another second, clearly filing that away for later.

“The missing patrol came back.”

That stopped him.

“Both of them?”

“Both. A little beaten up, confused, and apparently dumped close enough to the eastern road for another patrol to find them. Neither seems to know much. They were ambushed, knocked out, woke up somewhere unfamiliar and eventually got released.”

Levi's expression changed by barely anything.

Released.

After Eren had told him he didn't know where they were.

Coincidence was possible.

Levi was rapidly developing an allergy to coincidences where Eren Jaeger was concerned.

“When?”

Hanji frowned.

“Not long ago. Why?”

Levi looked toward the gates behind him.

Perhaps Eren genuinely hadn't known.

Perhaps someone had released the soldiers independently.

Or perhaps Jaeger had lied directly to his face and arranged their return the moment Levi walked out of the tavern.

At this point, Levi wouldn't have gambled a coin on any of the three.

“Levi?”

“Nothing.”

Hanji's eyes narrowed.

That word had never once successfully discouraged her.

“You've been missing for hours, you come back looking like someone dragged you through half the forest, you've got marks on your wrists, and suddenly you're very interested in exactly when two kidnapped guards were released.”

She stepped closer.

“What happened?”

Levi started walking.

“Long night.”

Hanji immediately followed.

“That's not an answer.”

“Good observation.”

“Were you captured?”

He kept walking.

“Levi.”

“Go bother the guards. They're the ones who need examining.”

“I'm going to bother them and you.”

Despite himself, Levi almost smiled.

Almost.

There would be reports in the morning. Questions. An investigation into the stolen crops, the attack, the missing soldiers and probably Levi's own disappearance.

And somewhere beyond the castle walls, Eren Jaeger would wake up knowing that Levi now held enough information to destroy whatever life he'd constructed for himself.

Levi hadn't promised him silence.

He hadn't promised him anything.

But he'd walked out without giving his name.

For tonight, that would have to be enough.

As he followed Hanji inside, Levi found his thoughts returning once more to green eyes behind a mask, a hand closing around his wrist in the forest, and the memory of Eren's thumb brushing the dirt from his cheek only hours earlier — gentle, thoughtless, almost strangely familiar.

That was perhaps the most irritating part of all. Even now, after everything that had happened, Levi couldn't quite reconcile that touch with the man who had watched him wake up bound to a chair.

Eren Jaeger seemed determined to be suspicious, infuriating, unexpectedly gentle... and increasingly impossible to dismiss.

The camp was over.

Unfortunately, Levi had the distinct feeling his involvement with Eren Jaeger wasn't.
