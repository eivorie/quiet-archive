Chapter VI

Eren

Eren was in his room when he was suddenly hit by a ray of sunlight directly in the face. He squinted his eyes.

“Are you going to sleep all day? We have to start setting up the stalls, and there’s a tournament happening here today. A day for celebration, food, and making a little money. Don’t be lazy…” Carla finished opening the window and noticed the scar on Eren, who was shirtless.

Eren noticed her gaze and didn’t wait for her to ask.

“It happened at the camp. I really don’t do well on trails. I got this little scratch as a souvenir.” He smiled.

Carla smiled back. “You’re a little distracted, to put it mildly… Should I get some arnica for you?”

“No, no, I already used some. I’ll get some ice. Arnica alone isn’t going to fix this.” He said, getting out of bed.

“Alright…” she said, studying Eren for a little longer before leaving.

After some time, Eren got dressed and went outside, walking through the village. Everyone was busy setting things up, hanging the torches, and preparing everything. They all seemed excited, but Eren remained concerned.

A few flashes from the previous night crossed his mind, along with a little suspicion. A little apprehension. But for some reason, Levi’s eyes kept coming back to his mind, interfering with his thoughts.

“Eren!” Armin came over and hugged him. “How are you?” He pulled Eren toward a more secluded corner.

“I have a lot to tell you, but… I’m fine,” Eren said.

Armin noticed how anxious Eren was and decided not to push him too much.

“We’re going to talk a lot tonight, with a drink in our hands. It’s alright. It seems Zeke is satisfied with the last harvest and hasn’t questioned anything else so far. We’re okay…”

Eren took a deep breath, but his mind still refused to shut off. Yet, seeing Armin’s enthusiasm, he decided to let it go.

“Let’s just enjoy the day. It’s the most we can do.” He said, though he didn’t even manage a smile.

Levi

Levi had managed perhaps two hours of sleep. It wasn't enough, but it would have to be.

By late morning, he was already crossing the castle courtyard again, freshly changed, the marks around his wrists hidden beneath his gloves and a considerably stronger cup of tea doing what sleep had failed to accomplish.

The missing soldiers had confirmed Hanji's account. Neither could identify their attackers. Both remembered being ambushed near the southern fields, waking somewhere dark, and eventually being left near the eastern road.

Convenient.

Levi had asked whether either of them had seen Eren. Neither had. Even more convenient. He stopped thinking about it before irritation ruined the little rest he'd managed to get.

The village tournament had complicated matters further. Cancelling it would cause panic over an incident the Crown couldn't yet explain, and hundreds of people were already expected to attend. Instead, several soldiers had been assigned to the village for the day.

Levi had volunteered before anyone could assign someone else. Needless to say, he preferred not to examine that decision too closely.

Security was a perfectly reasonable explanation.

The fact that Eren Jaeger happened to live there was irrelevant. Mostly.

By the time the village came into view, preparations were already well underway. Stalls lined the streets, ribbons and lanterns hung between buildings, and the smell of bread, roasted meat and something sweet drifted through the morning air. Children ran between adults carrying crates while merchants argued over spaces that had apparently belonged to their families since the beginning of civilization.

Levi slowed his horse.

This was different from the camp.

There, the villagers had been trainees. Representatives. People gathered temporarily under the supervision of the Crown.

Here, this was their world.

And somewhere inside it was a man who had apparently managed to become the leader of a group stealing from royal estates while still finding time to sell medicinal herbs and pretend he couldn't shoot a bow.

Levi dismounted near the central square.

“Captain.”

One of the guards approached.

“We've been told to keep the eastern and southern entrances covered. The tournament starts this afternoon. Nothing unusual so far.”

“Keep it that way. Don't start searching people without cause. We're here to prevent trouble, not create it.”

The soldier nodded and moved away.

Levi adjusted his gloves. He should inspect the perimeter, speak with the local guards, check the roads. Instead, his attention wandered across the square.

Once.

Then again.

Annoying.

He knew exactly who he was looking for. And apparently the universe had decided not to make him wait long.

A familiar figure stood farther down the street beside one of the stalls, partially obscured by people carrying baskets and wooden crates.

There he was.

Levi stopped.

For a moment, neither the theft nor the hideout came first. His eyes went automatically to Eren's side. The ribs.

He remembered the wince from the night before, the cotton pressed against bruised skin and the ridiculous satisfaction he'd felt seeing Eren had apparently listened to him about the ice.

Then common sense returned. Levi's expression flattened.

Don't start.

He had spent half the night wondering whether Eren had lied about the soldiers. He wasn't going to forget that because the idiot had followed one piece of medical advice.

Still...

Eren was here, walking around freely. Alive.

Levi hadn't realized until that moment that some irritating part of him had needed to confirm it.

He started toward the square.

Not directly toward Eren, that would be ridiculous.

He had work to do.

Unfortunately, that work appeared to be taking him in precisely the same direction.

Eren

Eren was finishing up helping with the setup of the stalls, greeting a few familiar faces as they passed by.

Armin showed up with some tools to help with the setup.

“I was told the royal soldiers were around,” Armin said, handing out the tools and helping Eren with the construction.

Eren stopped for a second and looked at Armin, slightly tense.

“What were they doing here?”

Armin looked at Eren and smiled. “Aren’t they always here for every festival, Eren? Did you lose your memory? Relax… They always inspect the area before tournaments and festivals. You know that.”

“Hmm…” Eren said, looking around but seeing no movement other than the villagers. “Makes sense… Hmm, Armin…”

“Yes…” Armin replied as he hammered a wooden stake into the ground, securing the part of the tent Eren was holding.

“Do you know if Levi was here?” he asked quickly, making Armin pause for a moment.

“No, Eren… Like I said, they mentioned the royal soldiers. Does it make a difference?” Armin asked.

“None.” He said. “Let’s do the other side. The sun will be going down soon, and I just want to be able to eat and have a drink.” He smiled faintly.

Armin nodded, and they continued working on the back of the tent.

Levi

Levi watched them for another moment before giving up on the ridiculous pretense that he had any reason to inspect that particular side of the square.

He crossed the remaining distance.

Eren had his back partially turned, holding one side of the tent while Armin worked on securing it. Levi's eyes moved over him once, quickly. Upright. Moving normally. No obvious sign that the bruising from the night before had gotten significantly worse.

That was all the attention he intended to give the matter.

“Jaeger.”

Levi stopped beside the structure and looked up at what Eren and Armin had managed so far.

His expression remained perfectly serious.

“Are you actually working, or is this another one of those things you're pretending to be bad at?”

There. Eren could decide whether that was an accusation or a joke. As a matter of fact, it was probably both.

Levi glanced toward Armin and gave him a brief nod of acknowledgment before his attention returned to Eren. Seeing him here, surrounded by villagers, tools in hand and sunlight on his face, was strangely disorienting after the masked figure from the night before.

Levi still hadn't quite decided what to do with that.

“Don't worry. I'm not here to drag you away in chains in front of half the village.”

His voice dropped enough that the words belonged primarily to Eren.

“Though considering how you chose to end our evening, you'd have very little room to complain about the hospitality.”

There was still anger there. Eren would have to be blind not to notice it. But Levi hadn't come over looking for a fight, and he certainly wasn't going to start interrogating him with Armin standing beside them.

Last night could wait until they were alone.

Levi's attention moved to the unfinished stall again, then toward the preparations spreading through the village. For all the questions still turning in his head, there was something almost absurd about standing here discussing kidnappings while someone nearby argued loudly about where to hang a string of lanterns.

His gaze returned to Eren.

“You owe me an explanation.”

Then, after the smallest pause:

“And a beer.”

That one carried the faintest trace of dry amusement.

“You promised me one before you took me sightseeing through the forest and had me drugged. I haven't forgotten.”

Levi adjusted one glove over his wrist, hiding what remained of the marks beneath it.

“I'm willing to postpone the explanation until you aren't surrounded by half your village.”

His eyes narrowed slightly.

“The beer, however, I'm collecting tonight.”

And perhaps that said more than Levi intended.

He could have returned to the castle after checking the village. He probably should have.

Instead, despite everything Eren had done, Levi was standing here informing him that there would be a later.

Eren

Eren heard the familiar voice. A thread of tension ran down the back of his neck and into his arms, sending a shiver through him. He felt nervous. Was Levi there just to announce that he had given Eren’s name to the royal family? He didn’t know, but Eren continued working as he listened to Levi.

Armin showed no reaction and went to the other side of the tent to finish setting it up.

Eren listened carefully to Levi’s words, and several times he felt his jaw tense. The mixture of sarcasm and bluntness—something very typical of Levi, and something Eren had already noticed even before everything that had happened.

Eren didn’t answer immediately. He simply smiled and politely greeted an older woman who was passing by.

“My lord, these chamomiles are beautiful!” she said, pointing at them.

“Aren’t they?” He smiled. “Would you like some? I can give you a handful, and the other handful will go to the soldier over there.” Eren pointed. “He’s very stressed.”

She laughed. “But you’re both much too young to be this stressed. But chamomile tea might help you…” She waited for him to give her his name.

“Captain Levi…” Eren said with a smile.

“It might help you, Captain Levi.” She smiled at Levi and waved before walking away.

Eren turned toward Levi and stared at him for a moment, as if quickly analyzing him. He seemed fine. Eren returned his focus to the task.

“I’ll keep my promises. A beer tonight, when the festival is underway. But for now, excuse me, I have to finish all of this.”

He turned around and almost letting a soft smirk, but didn’t.

Levi

Levi watched the woman walk away, then slowly turned his attention back to Eren.

Chamomile.

After everything that had happened, the bastard was prescribing him chamomile.

“How thoughtful.”

His eyes narrowed at the faint smirk Eren was doing a terrible job of hiding.

“I'll make sure to drink it before the next time you decide to drug me. Wouldn't want to be tense about it.”

Quiet enough for Armin not to catch the full meaning.

Eren had already turned back to his work, apparently satisfied that he'd dismissed Levi for the afternoon. Levi might have accepted that graciously if his eyes hadn't immediately landed on the section of canvas Eren had just secured.

He stared at it.

Then at Eren.

Then back at the canvas.

“Right. Wouldn't want to disturb you. You clearly need all the concentration you can get.”

Levi stepped closer and caught the edge of the fabric, pulling it free from where Eren had fastened it.

“You've put this on backwards.”

He flipped the fold over and handed it back to him.

“At this rate, that beer's going to be tomorrow morning.”

There was something satisfying about the brief return to normal irritation. No masks. No ropes. No men threatening executions. Just Eren being Eren and apparently incapable of assembling a fucking stall correctly.

Levi took a step back, giving the structure one final, judgmental inspection.

“Finish your masterpiece, Jaeger. I'll leave you to it.”

Then he continued across the square, leaving Eren with his tent, his chamomile, and hopefully enough remaining intelligence to figure out which way the canvas went.

Eren

Eren watched Levi and thought about interrupting him, but then he noticed his mistake. Eren sighed, almost rolling his eyes. He didn’t want to admit such an obvious mistake.

For a second, he forgot about the previous night and simply focused on Levi and his natural personality, which came with a touch of annoyance.

“I knew I was wrong, but you distracted me by saying things I didn’t need reminding of…” he said, watching Levi mount his horse again. “But thank you.” He said it quickly as he watched Levi ride away.

Eren took a deep breath and returned his focus to what he was doing.

A few hours passed, and it was already quite dark in the village. Torches and a few lanterns illuminated the paths, while the smell of food had grown even stronger. Music filled every corner. People seemed happy and excited. In some places, small performances were taking place, with attempts at magic that made some of the children smile.

Eren walked around, taking in the peacefulness of the village.

Levi

Levi had spent most of the evening doing exactly what he was supposed to do.

Checking the patrols stationed around the village, making sure none of the entrances had been left unattended, observing the crowd without interfering with it. So far, nothing justified the additional security. No suspicious movement, no disturbance beyond a few villagers who had already discovered their limits with ale.

For once, everything seemed remarkably ordinary.

He had eventually stopped near one of the quieter stalls when Petra found him.

— I was wondering how long you'd manage to avoid everyone.

Levi glanced at her.

— I wasn't aware I was trying.

— Of course not.

She fell into step beside him anyway, carrying a cup of something that smelled far too sweet. Petra had been asking more questions about the infirmary lately—what Hanji actually did there, how much medical knowledge soldiers were expected to have, whether someone trained primarily for combat could eventually move into healing work.

Levi answered between occasional dry remarks, but genuinely enough. She was serious about it. That alone made the conversation worth having.

— You'd have to study more than battlefield medicine, — he told her. — Knowing how to stop someone from bleeding to death isn't the same as knowing why they've been coughing for three weeks.

— I know that.

— Good. Because Hanji will make you learn the difference in exhausting detail.

Petra laughed.

— You make working with her sound terrible.

— It is.

— And yet you've been doing it for years.

— I make poor decisions occasionally.

Petra was midway through pointing out that this was perhaps the least convincing thing he'd ever said when Levi's attention shifted past her shoulder.

He saw Eren.

It happened annoyingly quickly.

One moment Levi was listening to Petra; the next his eyes had caught on a familiar figure moving between the lanterns farther down the path.

Eren looked different here.

Not substantially. Same clothes, same face, same infuriating tendency to attract Levi's attention without apparently doing anything to earn it. But there was an ease to the way he moved through the village, greeting people as he passed, occasionally stopping for a few words. This was clearly home in a way the camp never had been.

Levi realized Petra was still talking.

— ...and then Hanji said I could probably start—

— Petra.

She stopped.

Levi dragged his attention back to her.

— Sorry.

That alone earned him a suspicious look.

Levi Ackerman interrupting someone to apologize for not listening was apparently concerning enough to warrant investigation.

Petra followed the direction his eyes had gone.

— Oh.

Levi immediately disliked that oh.

— Don't.

Her mouth curved.

— I didn't say anything.

— Keep up the good work.

He handed her the cup he'd been holding.

— We'll finish this conversation later.

Petra accepted it, looking considerably too entertained now.

— Of course.

Levi ignored the tone entirely and stepped away.

He could have waited.

Eren had promised him the beer. Technically, Eren should have been the one looking for him.

Levi considered that for approximately three steps before deciding he didn't give a shit.

He moved through the crowd toward him.

After the previous night, there should probably have been some hesitation. Anger, certainly. Suspicion, absolutely. Enough unanswered questions to keep them occupied until sunrise.

All of that remained.

It simply didn't stop Levi from wanting to see him.

By the time he reached Eren, his expression had settled back into its usual composure.

— Jaeger.

His eyes briefly traveled over him before returning to his face.

— Starting to think keeping promises isn't one of your many hidden talents.

Eren

Eren spotted Levi in the distance. He couldn’t hear the conversation, but they both seemed quite interested in whatever they were discussing. Eren kept his eyes on the two of them for longer than necessary until the crowd trying to pass by bumped into him. Eren simply kept walking, greeting a few familiar faces with a smile and exchanging a few words.

When he heard Levi, he turned around and saw him standing right in front of him.

“You say that as if I wasn’t here. I was just waiting for you to finish your important conversation. At least, you seemed busy.”

He looked away slightly before meeting Levi’s eyes again.

“I don’t think you’re going to want to go to the tavern… so I guess we can grab a few bottles from the drinks stall and go somewhere else? What do you suggest?”

Levi

Levi's eyebrow rose slightly.

— Nothing was stopping you from coming over. Do I have to collect everything on my own now too?

There was no real irritation behind it. If anything, the fact that Eren had apparently been watching him long enough to decide his conversation with Petra looked important was mildly interesting. Levi chose not to investigate that particular detail. Yet.

His gaze shifted toward the tavern, already crowded enough that voices and laughter spilled through the open doors, then back to Eren.

— Somewhere else.

That decision required no thought whatsoever.

— Unless you want the entire village listening when I ask why you're secretly a lunatic who drugs soldiers and keeps them tied up in tavern basements. Your choice.

Levi started toward the drinks stall, apparently trusting Eren to follow.

He glanced sideways at him.

— Pick somewhere quiet. Preferably somewhere I won't get shot at this time.

A faint smirk touched his mouth.

— Think you can manage that, Jaeger?

Eren

“I’m trying very hard not to roll my eyes, but you’re not helping.”

He followed Levi.

“I told you I owed you a beer before last night, so keep your curiosity to yourself. You’re safe and sound.”

He looked Levi up and down before turning toward the stall.

They both picked out a few bottles.

“I think the safest place is a sort of dead-end street we have on the other side. It also has a view of the lake, so you don’t have to worry, you’ll have somewhere to run or swim if necessary.” He said it in an ironic tone.

Levi

Levi glanced at him as they left the stall.

— A dead end with access to deep water. Somehow you keep making this evening sound more appealing.

He adjusted the bottles in his hand and followed Eren through the crowd.

— I'm not swimming, though. If I end up in that lake, you're coming with me.

The noise of the festival gradually softened as they moved farther away, lanterns becoming less frequent along the path.

Levi looked sideways at Eren.

— Nice place, by the way. The village. Not your carefully selected murder alley.

His eyes moved briefly toward the lights reflecting across the lake.

— You grew up here?

Eren

Eren looked around and then glanced over at Levi.

“I wouldn’t do that on the day of the festival. That would be a failure on my part.” He joked about Levi’s comment about “Murder Alley.”

They eventually arrived, and Eren sat down on the ground. There wasn’t much light, but the glow from the festival kept the surroundings from becoming completely dark, combined with the reflection of the lake.

“Yeah… I’ve been here forever. My entire childhood was spent in this village.” He watched the lights for a moment before extending his hand, asking for a bottle.

Levi

Levi handed him one of the bottles before taking a seat beside him, leaving enough space to be comfortable without putting any unnecessary distance between them. He opened his own and took a drink, his eyes settling on the lights scattered across the lake.

— That explains a few things.

He let the accusation hang there for a second before adding:

— Half the village seems to know you. I couldn't walk ten steps earlier without hearing someone mention Jaeger for one reason or another.

There was something different about seeing Eren here. At the camp, Levi had known him primarily through what he could do—how quickly he learned, the inconsistencies in his abilities, the questions he asked. Here, every few minutes someone greeted him, smiled at him, called his name. It filled in pieces Levi hadn't realized were missing.

He tipped the bottle lightly against his knee. His gaze remained on the lake for another moment before shifting back to Eren.

— So what were you like?

A faint trace of amusement appeared.

— And think carefully before answering. I'm trying to imagine you at eight years old, and so far all I've got is smaller, louder, and probably covered in dirt.
