Chapter IV

Eren

It was night.

The sounds of the forest echoed through the darkness, mingling with distant voices and the crackling of bonfires scattered throughout the camp.

There was barely any light beyond the flames, lanterns, and torches that cast faint, flickering shadows across the surroundings.

Pixis watched attentively, making a few notes on a piece of paper beside him.

Two other instructors stood nearby—Levi and Hange—quietly observing the students.

Falco drew his bow, carefully aligning the arrow with the target fixed to the tree.

Without hesitation, he released it.

The arrow flew straight and true, striking almost dead center.

“Good attempt,” Pixis murmured, almost to himself.

“One of the best we have here.”

He glanced down at his notes before raising his voice.

“Last student of the night. Eren Jaeger, step forward.”

Eren approached the designated spot.

“Feet on the yellow mark. Focus on the target. Keep your grip light. Bow at shoulder height.”

Eren positioned himself accordingly and took a deep breath.

His gaze settled on the target.

One eye narrowed as he focused his vision through the other, locking onto the small red dot at the very center of the target.

He pulled the string back.

For a moment, everything around him seemed to disappear.

Then he released.

The arrow struck the target—just above the bullseye.

“Ugh…”

A frustrated sound escaped him as he lowered the bow, though he remained standing in position.

“Hmph… Eren,” Pixis muttered to the other instructors.

“He can ace every other discipline, but when it comes to archery, he’s absolutely terrible.”

“Can I try one more time?” Eren asked, turning his head toward Pixis.

“Not now. You had the entire day to practice. Everyone gets one shot.”

Pixis glanced toward the target before looking back at him.

“At this rate, we may have to admit that the only thing you’re good at is making recipes with medicinal plants. Perhaps that’s where your talents lie, young man.”

His tone was stern, as usual.

“Hey, everyone! Today’s lesson is over! You’re dismissed. Head back to your tents and get some rest.”

Slowly, the students began making their way down the trail toward the tents, their voices fading into the darkness.

Everyone except Eren.

He remained where he was, picked up another arrow, and tried again.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Four.

None of them hit the mark.

Eren lowered the bow and stared at the target in silence for a few seconds before letting out a frustrated sigh.

“It sucks…”

Levi

The other trainees began dispersing toward the camp, relieved that the day's exercises were finally over.

Conversations resumed around the fires, boots crunching over dry leaves as the instructors remained behind to gather the equipment.

Levi's eyes followed Eren a moment longer.

Every exercise that required endurance, balance or close combat had come naturally to him.

Archery did not.

He picked up the bow Eren had just lowered down to check the target, and walked over without announcing himself.

“Again.”

His voice was calm, leaving little room for discussion.

When Eren took the bow back, Levi stepped behind him.

Close enough to reach, far enough not to crowd him.

His fingers rested lightly beneath Eren's elbow and lifted it barely an inch.

“There.”

He adjusted the angle of his wrist with the same precision.

“No more.”

His hand withdrew.

“I spent the first week thinking you were making yourself look worse than you are. Turns out you've simply been taught badly.”

There wasn't a hint of mockery in his tone.

If anything, it sounded almost... suspicious on Eren's behalf.

Levi folded his arms.

“Which surprises me.”

His eyes lingered briefly on the broad shoulders in front of him before returning to the target.

“You don't look like someone who grew up relying on other people to feed him.”

He marked a pause.

“So who hunted?”

The question was simple.

Matter-of-fact.

“If it wasn't you.”

Eren

Eren kept his focus on the target, but he didn’t release the arrow, allowing himself to be repositioned by Levi.

“Talking like that, you’d think people at the castle actually had to hunt for their food.”

Eren smiled faintly.

“Fortunately, we have good merchants to take care of that, because if it were up to me, apparently everyone would starve.”

He released the arrow.

This time, it landed a little closer to the target, though still not directly on it.

“Damn it…” he muttered in frustration.

“He really is right. Maybe I should’ve just stuck to the medicinal lessons. I don’t even know what I’m doing here.”

He lowered the bow and sat down on one of the logs near the fire, turning his gaze toward Levi.

“How long did it take you to become good at something?”

Levi

Levi raised an eyebrow.

The question lingered longer than it should have.

He had never been naturally good at anything.

There had simply never been room for failure.

His mother had died before he was old enough to remember her voice clearly.

The castle had taken him in from the streets afterward—not out of kindness, but because another pair of hands could always be put to use.

Everything he knew had been learned the same way: repetition, bruises, mistakes, and the certainty that no one else would fix them for him.

None of it reached his face.

He stepped behind Eren once more.

“Stand up.”

Once Eren was back in position, Levi reached for the bow.

His hand settled lightly over Eren's wrist, turning it by a fraction.

“No.”

His other hand found Eren's elbow, lifting it just enough.

“There.”

He circled him once, studying his stance from the front before returning to his side.

“You're fighting the bow.”

Another small adjustment.

“It isn't a contest of strength.”

Levi leaned in just enough for the next words not to carry beyond the two of them.

“As for the answer to your question...”

His voice was low, close enough that Eren could feel it more than hear it.

“I don't know.”

His eyes shifted briefly toward Eren's profile.

“I was trained.”

He stepped back as though nothing unusual had happened.

“Again.”

Eren

Eren followed the instructions, keeping his eyes focused on the tree, but with every adjustment, he seemed to lose a little more of his concentration.

Perhaps he was simply too tired.

He relaxed his shoulders and sighed before releasing the arrow, once again landing near the target—better than before, but never quite on it.

“I can’t concentrate… I think I should take a break for today.”

He looked at Levi.

“But for the extra minutes of lessons, you can stop by the tavern, and I’ll consider whether you’ve earned a free drink or not.”

He almost smiled, but didn’t.

“You must be tired too, aren’t you?”

Levi

Levi glanced briefly toward the tavern lights before shaking his head.

“Another time.”

Whether he meant it or not was impossible to tell.

A quiet voice drew his attention.

“Excuse me...? I need help with this.”

Petra stood beside another trainee, a strip of linen wrapped awkwardly around the young man's hand.

Levi stepped in, loosened the bandage with practiced fingers, and began wrapping it again with quick, precise movements.

As Petra instinctively reached to hold the loose end in place, her fingertips brushed against his.

A faint flush crept across her cheeks, though she didn't pull away.

Levi adjusted her grip with the slightest pressure of his own hand, secured the final turn of the bandage, then withdrew the moment it held on its own.

Without another word, he stepped aside.

Petra watched him leave for a brief second before turning toward Eren.

A small, almost embarrassed smile tugged at the corner of her lips.

Levi's gaze returned to Eren, as he gave him a slight nod.

“Go easy on the drinks.”

His eyes drifted briefly toward the target still standing at the edge of the clearing.

“Get some rest. I'd rather not spend another evening correcting the same mistake.”

He didn't wait for a reply.

He was already crossing the clearing toward Hanji, the two of them exchanging a few quiet words before disappearing into the shifting firelight, leaving Eren with the bow, the target... and one more reason to return tomorrow.

Levi

The camp had not yet awakened.

Only a handful of watchfires still burned, their embers glowing beneath a pale sky that had only just begun to surrender the stars. The forest remained quiet, interrupted only by the occasional crack of cooling wood and the distant murmur of someone changing the night watch.

Levi opened his eyes before dawn, as he always did.

For a moment, he remained still beneath the rough wool blanket, listening.

Silence was useful. It revealed what noise often concealed.

Outside, a horse shifted in its tether. Somewhere nearby, metal clicked softly against leather as one of the guards adjusted his equipment.

Another day.

He sat up and reached automatically for the neatly folded clothes resting beside his bedroll. The movements required no thought anymore. Shirt first. Harness. The dark jacket buttoned with the same precision every morning. Belts checked. Blades secured. Nothing extravagant. Nothing forgotten.

The routine left enough room for his mind to wander.

For weeks they had welcomed representatives from villages across the realm, teaching them how to preserve herbs, dress wounds, purify water, recognize poisonous plants, and defend themselves.

Useful knowledge.

Necessary knowledge.

Yet every lesson carried the same contradiction.

They taught people how to survive without truly changing the reasons survival had become difficult in the first place.

Knowledge could stretch limited resources.

It could not replace them.

The fields surrounding the southern estates had confirmed as much.

The selective harvesting had continued.

Never enough to empty a storehouse.

Never careless enough to expose whoever was responsible.

Just enough to remind him that someone, somewhere, had already concluded that waiting politely would solve nothing.

Levi did not entirely disagree with the conclusion.

Only with the method.

He fastened the final buckle around his wrist and reached for his gloves.

His thoughts drifted, unexpectedly, toward one of the trainees.

Eren Jaeger.

Not because he stood out in combat. Many of them possessed strengths of their own.

It was the inconsistency.

The young man adapted quickly, read situations well, learned faster than most, and fought with an instinct that could not be taught in a classroom.

Then someone placed a bow in his hands...

...and it was as though those instincts vanished.

Levi frowned faintly.

He had watched enough soldiers over the years to recognize the difference between inability and hesitation.

With Eren, he still wasn't certain which he was looking at.

The possibility lingered longer than he liked.

What kind of man accepted ignorance so easily?

Everything he had observed suggested Eren wasn't that kind of man.

Unless the failure itself served a purpose.

Levi dismissed the thought before it settled too deeply.

Assumptions were dangerous.

Facts were useful.

This morning would provide more of those.

He stepped outside the tent into the crisp dawn air. Thin mist still clung to the clearing where the training grounds had been marked out the day before. Wooden practice weapons waited in orderly racks, untouched.

Hand-to-hand combat.

That, at least, would leave little room to hide.

Levi adjusted the cuff of one glove and made his way across the waking camp.

For the first time since arriving, he found himself almost curious to see how the morning would unfold.

Eren

Eren walked along the trails, exchanging a few words with some of his peers. He was already dressed and ready for the day, though the exhaustion in his eyes betrayed the restless night he had barely managed to sleep through.

The last day of camp.

He could say that every day had been worthwhile—countless lessons, plenty of theory, a great deal of practice, but almost no solutions.

Eren positioned himself near the training grounds, waiting for the instructors to appear. He glanced around as if searching for something, and then his eyes landed on the wooden practice weapons.

“Hand-to-hand combat,” Falco said aloud, breaking Eren’s train of thought.

“I’m starting to get tired. I’m glad this is the last day. It’s not like fighting people from the village is difficult,” he said arrogantly.

“You don’t even know who we’re going to fight. Imagine if they put you against Captain Levi. Would you say the same thing?” Gabi laughed under her breath.

“I don’t know why you’re all so afraid of them. It’s ridiculous…”

Falco’s trembling voice made Eren smile.

“I don’t think it’s about fear. It’s about ability. Levi grew up within the royal court. He was trained from a young age and had access to the best training to become the soldier he is today. And because he has their trust…” Eren said, watching him from a distance.

“So, would you say the same about all of them? Because he wasn’t the only one,” Falco cut him off.

“He wasn’t the only one, but discipline is the secret to standing out. Something you definitely don’t have when you sleep until eleven in the morning.” Eren chuckled, making Gabi laugh along with him.

“You can’t say much yourself,” Falco complained, crossing his arms as he watched the instructors approach.

Levi

Levi caught the tail end of the conversation as he approached the training ground, Falco’s complaint carrying easily through the morning air.

He said nothing about it.

His gaze passed over the small group instead, taking in their posture, their equipment, the lingering traces of sleep on more than one face.

“I hope you’re warmed up.”

That was enough to straighten a few backs.

Levi stopped beside the rack of wooden weapons and folded his arms.

“If you still have enough energy to argue about who sleeps the longest, you have enough energy to work. So quit talking and pay attention.”

The amusement around the group died quickly.

Good.

“Today we’re covering close combat. Not a duel, and not an excuse to beat the hell out of each other.”

He stepped into the marked training area.

“Most fights outside a training ground aren’t clean. You lose your weapon. Someone gets inside your reach. You get grabbed before you have time to draw anything. At that distance, strength matters less than knowing what to do with the other person’s balance.”

His eyes moved over the trainees before settling, deliberately, on Eren.

“Jaeger. Come here.”

There was nothing unusual in his tone.

At least, Levi hoped there wasn’t.

Eren was an obvious enough choice. He was quick, physically capable, and had shown throughout the past weeks that he understood instructions without needing them repeated three times.

Those were perfectly reasonable explanations.

They also happened to be convenient ones.

Levi had spent too much time watching the strange contradiction that was Eren Jaeger. The instincts were there. He had seen them surface in everything except archery—brief moments of precision and awareness that didn't quite belong to someone whose experience was supposedly limited to whatever training his village could provide.

Perhaps this would settle it.

“You’re attacking.”

Levi waited until Eren stood opposite him, then gestured for the others to move closer.

“Watch his shoulders and hips. Not his hands. Hands can lie about where an attack is going. The rest of the body usually doesn’t.”

Then, to Eren:

“Grab me.”

He gave him a second.

“Properly, Jaeger. I’m not going to break because you touch me.”

Once Eren had a hold of him, Levi moved.

Not quickly.

That would defeat the purpose.

One hand closed around Eren’s wrist while Levi stepped into his space rather than away from it. His shoulder came close against Eren’s chest, their bodies briefly aligned as Levi turned, changing the angle of Eren’s arm and shifting his center of gravity.

“This is where most people make the first mistake. They pull.”

Levi looked toward the others, though he remained exactly where he was, Eren still caught in the position.

“Don’t. If someone stronger than you has a solid grip, pulling turns it into a contest you might lose.”

He adjusted Eren’s wrist by a fraction, then placed his other hand against his upper arm.

“Move toward the weakest point of the hold instead. Take the space they think belongs to them.”

A turn of his hips, a controlled shift of weight, and Eren was forced half a step off balance.

Levi stopped there.

He could have finished the movement easily.

Instead, something registered.

Eren had compensated.

Barely.

A minute adjustment of his footing, instinctive enough that most people watching probably hadn't noticed it at all.

Levi had.

His eyes lifted to Eren’s.

Interesting.

For one brief moment, the lesson disappeared from his attention.

There it was again.

That irritating little suggestion that Eren knew more than he showed.

Levi released him and stepped back.

“Again.”

This time his attention settled entirely on Eren.

“And don’t make it easy for me.”

The words could have belonged entirely to the lesson.

The look Levi gave him did not.

He wanted resistance.

More importantly, he wanted to see what Eren did when he stopped having time to think about what he was supposed to know.

Eren

Eren was slightly out of breath. He took a step back and stared at Levi after he released him. It wasn’t simply a matter of strength, but of the skill Levi possessed—the way he meticulously analyzed every movement.

Eren rolled his shoulders and moved forward. He tried to hook his leg around Levi’s to throw him off balance, but the attempt failed. Levi caught Eren by the arms, causing him to nearly lose his footing.

“Move toward the weakest point, you said, right?” Eren said quietly, looking into Levi’s eyes as he struggled to break free. For a few seconds, Levi’s breath mingled with Eren’s.

“What’s your weak point?” he asked with a sigh, twisting Levi’s arm behind his back and holding him there with difficulty.

A few whispers could be heard around them.

Eren held on with everything he had until Levi caught him off guard with a sweep of his leg, throwing him off balance and making him lose control.

“Nice try, Eren. Three seconds of skill,” Falco laughed.

Eren didn’t move. He simply lay there, breathing heavily, frustration evident in his expression.

Levi

The question earned Eren something rare.

A smirk.

Small, fleeting, but unmistakably there.

For all the effort Eren was putting into keeping Levi’s arm behind his back, he still had enough breath left to ask questions.

“Figure it out.”

Levi gave him no more than that.

A shift of weight, one precise sweep, and the hold disappeared as quickly as Eren had managed to secure it.

For a moment after Eren hit the ground, Levi remained standing over him.

Three seconds.

Falco could laugh all he wanted. Levi knew exactly how long three seconds could be in a real fight.

More importantly, Eren had listened.

He had taken a principle explained to him less than a minute earlier and immediately tried to turn it against the person who had taught it to him. The execution had been imperfect, but the decision hadn't been.

Levi's eyes narrowed slightly.

Again.

That same irritating feeling.

Not proof. Not yet.

But enough to keep his curiosity alive.

Falco's laughter broke through his thoughts.

“Nice try, Eren. Three seconds of skill.”

Levi's gaze shifted toward him.

“Three seconds longer than you've lasted against me.”

The whispers around them died immediately.

Levi looked back down at Eren and extended a hand.

“And he got my arm behind my back.”

There was no praise in the way he said it, merely fact. Somehow, coming from Levi, that carried more weight.

“He saw an opening and used it. That's the point of the damn exercise.”

When Eren took his hand, Levi closed his grip firmly around his and pulled him back onto his feet. Momentum brought Eren closer than expected, leaving barely any space between them for a brief second.

Levi didn't immediately step away.

His eyes searched Eren's face instead, catching the frustration still sitting there.

“Quit looking like someone killed your horse. You weren't supposed to beat me.”

A beat.

That faint smirk returned.

“Not yet, anyway.”

Only then did Levi release his hand and turn toward the others.

“Pair up. Similar size if you can manage it. One attacks, one breaks the hold. Switch after five attempts.”

Movement began around the clearing as everyone started choosing partners.

Levi pointed toward Falco without even looking at him.

“And since you've got enough breath left to comment on everyone else's performance, you can demonstrate next if you'd like.”

A few badly concealed laughs followed.

Levi finally looked at him.

“No?”

He waited just long enough to make the answer obvious.

“Then worry about your own footing.”

His attention swept across the rest of them.

“Nobody here improves by making someone else look worse. If your partner fails, show them where. If they catch you, figure out why. You're here to learn, not measure whose ego takes up the most room.”

He stepped out of the center to give them space, but his attention drifted back to Eren almost immediately.

Three seconds.

Levi flexed the arm Eren had managed to trap behind him, more thoughtful than bothered.

For someone who had spent the previous afternoon looking almost helpless with a bow, Jaeger had adapted remarkably fast once another person was standing within reach.

Perhaps too fast.

Levi folded his arms.

He'd watch.

And this time, he intended to pay very close attention.

Eren

Eren listened carefully to Levi’s words, unable to suppress a small smirk when he heard what he said to Falco. He shot the boy a quick glance, earning an eye roll in return. Eren almost laughed, but decided it wouldn’t be appropriate for the moment.

He rolled his neck in a slow stretch, thinking about how a simple, well-executed movement could be far more important than strength.

A few hours passed. A few more training sessions were completed, followed by dinner to bring the final day of camp to an end.

Everyone was talking amongst themselves. Eren had finished eating, while the instructors seemed to be gathered in conversation on the other side of the camp. His eyes wandered across the grounds.

Everyone was already on the verge of leaving.

Several tents had already been taken down. Eren had his backpack over his shoulders when he noticed Levi helping dismantle one of the last remaining tents.

He walked over and pulled out one of the wooden stakes holding the tent down on the other side.

“You could ask for help. You don’t always have to do everything by yourself.”

That was all Eren said before continuing to help, without waiting for permission.

Levi

Levi glanced toward the stake disappearing from the ground, then toward Eren.

“That's the problem with people who like things done properly. Asking for help usually means doing it twice.”

The remark came easily, but Levi made no attempt to stop him.

Instead, he moved to the opposite side of the tent and loosened another rope, watching from the corner of his eye as Eren worked without needing instructions.

“Since you're already interfering, fold that side inward. Not like that—”

Levi stepped closer and caught the edge of the canvas before Eren could move it.

“Here.”

His hand landed beside Eren's on the heavy fabric, their fingers briefly brushing as he pulled one corner across the other. It was an unnecessarily small distance between them for something that could have been explained from where he'd been standing.

Levi noticed.

Apparently, that didn't make him move.

“Otherwise you'll trap air inside and we'll spend ten minutes fighting with it.”

Together they lowered the canvas, forcing the remaining air out before folding it again. It was strangely easy. Eren followed directions quickly when he wanted to, and Levi found himself remembering the morning.

The arm behind his back.

Three seconds.

The question spoken close enough that Levi had felt the words as much as heard them.

What's your weak point?

His mouth threatened to curve.

“You did well today.”

It came without warning, even to him.

Levi straightened, gathering two of the wooden stakes before continuing.

“Better than yesterday, anyway. Although that's not difficult considering your performance with a bow.”

There.

Much safer territory.

He handed Eren the remaining stakes, but his gaze stayed on him.

“Close combat suits you.”

Perhaps too well.

Levi had watched him throughout the afternoon. Eren made mistakes, certainly, but rarely the same one twice. Once someone caught him with a movement, he adjusted. Once he understood where weight shifted or balance failed, his body seemed to remember before his mind had finished processing it.

That kind of instinct usually came from repetition.

Levi wasn't going to accuse him of anything based on instinct alone.

Not tonight.

Around them, the camp was disappearing piece by piece. Voices carried through the clearing as people called their goodbyes, packs were thrown over shoulders, horses prepared for the road.

Levi looked at the backpack Eren was already wearing.

“Heading straight home?”

It was a perfectly ordinary question.

Still, Levi found himself adding before Eren could answer:

“Or are you planning to disappear before I get another chance to watch you embarrass yourself with a weapon?”

A faint smirk appeared.

“Because I'm still not convinced about that bow.”

There was teasing in it, but also an invitation hidden neatly underneath.

The camp might be over.

Levi's curiosity, unfortunately, wasn't.

Eren

“If you’re not convinced, I challenge you to spend a week in this forest with me and put me in charge of hunting our food. I don’t think you’d have the courage. I hope you’re vegetarian.” Eren laughed quietly as he lifted one hand to Levi’s face, brushing his thumb lightly over his cheek.

“I don’t know how you managed it, but you had some dirt there. I guess you’re not quite as skilled at putting up tents as you are at everything else. But I’ll keep that a secret.”

He glanced toward the fire before turning back to Levi.

“Thank you for the lessons. I believe they’ll be of great use to all of us.”

Eren was about to say more when he slowly heard the sound of several horses approaching. He lifted his head.

“Do you know who they are?”

He watched as Levi stood up and followed suit, remaining beside him. In the distance, three royal guards were approaching. Eren recognized them by their uniforms.

They brought their horses to a stop.

“Soldier Levi, part of the castle’s crops has just been stolen. We have soldiers searching the area, but it doesn’t seem to have been very peaceful. Two of our soldiers are missing,” the guard said, slightly out of breath. “We need your help finding them. We’ll inform the others, and we’ll be leaving in ten minutes.”

Eren watched the soldiers ride away as Levi began gathering the remaining equipment from the ground. Almost involuntarily, Eren reached out and caught Levi’s hand.

“Levi, don’t you think it would be safer for you to conduct the search tomorrow? You should stay here tonight. You look exhausted, and I don’t think this is a good idea.”

Levi

For perhaps a second too long, Levi looked at the hand around his.

It was the second unexpected thing Eren had done in less than a minute.

The first had been brushing dirt from his face as though touching him required no more consideration than adjusting a crooked strap.

Levi had been too surprised to react then.

Now there were more important things to think about.

“Worried about me already, Jaeger?”

There was the faintest trace of amusement in his voice as his eyes lifted to Eren's.

“Careful. People might start thinking those lessons made you sentimental.”

But the humor didn't last.

Levi looked toward the road where the guards had disappeared, his expression sharpening.

Stolen crops.

Again.

Except this time two soldiers were missing.

That changed things.

Selective theft was one thing. Whoever had been taking supplies until now had been careful—deliberate, even. Enough to matter, never enough to invite an immediate military response.

If violence had entered the equation, either something had gone wrong...

...or Levi had misunderstood the situation entirely.

He looked back at Eren.

And there was something else.

Stay here tonight.

Perhaps it was concern. Eren certainly looked sincere enough.

But Levi had learned a long time ago that sincerity and innocence weren't the same thing.

Only moments earlier, Eren had confidently offered to keep the two of them alive in these woods for seven days.

Levi's gaze narrowed as he adjusted the strap securing the remaining equipment.

“So how well do you know these woods?”

The question was casual.

His attention wasn't.

He watched Eren's face carefully, searching for that fractional hesitation people rarely knew they gave away.

“Because you're right about one thing. Going into unfamiliar forest at night looking for two missing men isn't particularly smart.”

He paused.

“Going with someone who knows it isn't quite as stupid.”

Levi let the implication settle between them.

Then he looked down at Eren's hand still around his before raising an eyebrow.

“Congratulations. You may have just volunteered yourself.”

He could already imagine the objections.

Levi gently freed his hand, only to catch Eren's wrist for a moment before letting it go entirely.

“I'm not ordering you to come. This camp is finished. You're free to go home.”

That distinction mattered. Whatever suspicions Levi had, Eren wasn't one of his soldiers.

“But if you know these trails as well as you're implying, you could save us time. We find the missing soldiers, figure out what happened, and get out.”

His gaze shifted toward the dark line of trees. Ten minutes. Not much time.

And there was another problem Levi couldn't quite dismiss.

Eren had looked exhausted that morning too.

Dragging an untrained civilian into a potentially dangerous search because Levi happened to find him suspicious would be reckless.

He glanced at him again.

“Assuming your legendary woodland expertise extends beyond threatening me with starvation.”

The dry remark bought Levi another second to think.

“Your choice, Jaeger. Stay here where it's safe like you suggested...”

His eyes held Eren's.

“...or show me why you're so convinced I'd survive seven days in there with you.”

Eren

Eren didn’t pay much attention to Levi’s assumptions. He let out a deep sigh and glanced around before his eyes returned to Levi.

“I don’t know everything about it, but I can’t say I don’t know enough. Looking for medicinal plants doesn’t only happen in the fields. There are plenty of species and resources in the forest that can be used for construction. And, well, I may be terrible with artillery, but at least I’m strong enough to carry things. I suppose I’ve always been good for that.”

He hesitated, but then picked up his backpack and simply nodded.

“I want to help. I need to prove that I’m not made just to live in a cabin making medicine. If this makes me useful, then I’ll go with you.”

He took a step forward.

“Let’s go.”

Levi

Levi studied him for a moment.

There it was again.

That strange need to prove something.

He didn't know to whom—himself, his family, the people around him—but Eren had said it with enough conviction that Levi doubted there was any point trying to talk him out of coming now.

“Fine.”

He reached for his cloak and fastened it over his shoulders.

“But we're getting something straight first.”

Levi stepped in front of Eren before he could start toward the horses.

“Knowing the forest doesn't make you one of my soldiers. If something happens, you don't play hero. You don't run ahead because you saw something interesting, you don't disappear because you think you've found a shortcut, and you don't pick a fight with whoever might be out there just to prove you're useful.”

His eyes stayed firmly on Eren's.

“If I tell you to stop, you stop. If I tell you to get behind me, you do it. No arguments.”

Then the severity eased by a fraction.

“You've already proved you're capable, Jaeger. Don't ruin my assessment by doing something stupid on the last night.”

Levi turned away before the words could sound too much like reassurance.

“And if you get yourself killed after promising to feed me for seven days, I'll consider that false advertising.”

He collected the last of his equipment and started toward the horses.

By then, the other soldiers had begun gathering near the edge of camp. The atmosphere had changed entirely. Whatever fatigue and relief had accompanied the end of training were gone, replaced by hurried preparations and clipped exchanges.

Levi requested what information they had while checking the fastening on his saddle.

The missing men had been part of a patrol sent toward the southern fields after signs of another theft were discovered. Their tracks had led toward the forest. Another group had followed, found evidence of a struggle near the tree line, then lost the trail once the ground became harder beneath the canopy.

Not much.

But enough for a starting point.

Levi mounted, then glanced down at Eren.

“You're with me.”

Partly because Eren knew the area.

Partly because if there was something in these woods Eren wasn't telling him, Levi preferred having him where he could see him.

He kept the second reason to himself.

The group left camp several minutes later.

Once they passed the southern fields, the remaining torchlight behind them disappeared quickly. The forest swallowed sound differently at night. Hooves sank into damp earth, branches shifted overhead, and the lanterns carried by the soldiers illuminated only fragments of the path before darkness closed behind them again.

Levi rode near the front, Eren beside him.

After several minutes, he slowed his horse near a fork in the trail.

One path continued south, broad enough for carts.

The other narrowed sharply between the trees.

Levi looked between them before turning toward Eren.

“Alright, forest expert.”

A trace of dry amusement touched his expression.

“Earn your promotion. Which way?”
