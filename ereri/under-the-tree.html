Under the Tree

Levi — POV

Levi liked the tree because it didn’t ask him anything.

It stood at the far edge of the schoolyard, where the grass grew a little taller and the noise softened into something he could ignore.

The branches were low. The shade stayed cool. The ground beneath it felt familiar.

Levi ate there every day.

Alone.

He sat with his back against the trunk, lunchbox on his knees, eating slowly, carefully, like everything had its place.

No one bothered him.

At least, no one was supposed to.

Eren had tried.

The first time, he’d just stood a few steps away, holding his lunch like he wasn’t sure what to do with it.

“Hey,” he’d said. “You always sit here?”

Levi hadn’t looked up.

The second time, Eren sat down anyway.

Too close. Too loud.

Levi closed his lunchbox without a word and left.

The third time, Eren followed him halfway across the playground, talking the entire time about a cartoon Levi didn’t care about.

Levi stopped, turned around, and said flatly:

“Go away.”

So Eren did.

For a while.

After that, Eren tried from a distance.

Sitting at another table. Waving sometimes. Talking too loudly in Levi’s direction like that might count as conversation.

It didn’t.

Levi went back to his tree. Alone. Like he wanted.

Until one day, Eren had had enough.

It was a Tuesday. Levi remembered because Tuesdays were soup days, and soup was the worst thing to carry.

He had just sat down when footsteps crunched through the grass.

Levi sighed.

“I said—”

“I know,” Eren cut in, already sitting down. “You say it every time.”

Levi turned sharply.

“I don’t want—”

“I’m eating here,” Eren said, opening his lunchbox. “You can move if you want.”

Levi stared at him.

Eren stared back, completely unbothered.

The tree didn’t change. The world didn’t end.

“You’re annoying,” Levi said.

“I get that a lot,” Eren replied, grinning.

Levi waited for him to leave.

He didn’t.

Eren ate noisily. Kicked his feet. Commented on his food like it was a performance.

“This apple is disappointing,” he announced.

“Then don’t eat it,” Levi muttered.

“I might later.”

Levi frowned, irritated — but he didn’t move.

After a while, Eren got quieter.

He noticed things. The way Levi ate in small bites. The way he flinched when someone yelled nearby.

Eren lowered his voice without being told.

That was new.

They didn’t talk much that day.

Or the next.

But Eren came back anyway.

Every lunch.

And Levi didn’t tell him to leave again.

The other kids tried once or twice.

Too curious. Too loud.

Levi packed up and walked away immediately.

Eren watched them go, then looked at Levi.

“What?”

“They’re too much,” Levi said.

Eren smiled, pleased.

“Good.”

After a while, it was understood.

The tree was Levi’s.

And Eren’s too.

Only Eren.

One day, Eren leaned back against the trunk, arms crossed, satisfied.

“You know,” he said, “I think I’m special.”

Levi glanced at him.

“You’re loud.”

“Exactly,” Eren said proudly. “And you still let me sit here.”

Levi looked back at his lunch.

“Don’t tell anyone.”

Eren’s grin was unstoppable.

“I won,” he whispered.
